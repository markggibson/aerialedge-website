**Two vertical apparatus you climb, wrap into and drop from – and a beginner-friendly class on each to start this week.**

Aerial rope and aerial silks are sister apparatus, yet like all siblings, they are entirely different as well as being related! Rope, sometimes called corde lisse, is a single soft rope. Silks, sometimes called tissu, is two lengths of fabric. On both, you climb, wrap yourself into the material and drop from it under control. Neither needs you to arrive strong. You get strong from doing it, and our instructors take complete beginners through every wrap and climb safely.

On rope, what often hooks people is dynamic swing, and release-and-catch drops – that means using momentum so you flow through the air between shapes. You'll climb and wrap first, so you can rest mid-air looking like a pro, when you're actually just resting on a well-built knot. Then come drops. You release the wrap and it looks like you're falling, but the wrap does its job and you stop, safely, in control. It's nothing like the rope in a school gym hall.

Silks works the same instincts on different material. With two fabric tails instead of one rope, you can wrap both around yourself at once – a cocoon-like wrap, or a split where the fabric falls either side of you. That's the shape most people picture when they think of silks. You climb, wrap to a secure hold, then unwrap or drop from it. Dramatic to watch and cool to do. Most of the skill is in how you wrap, and how you use strength and dynamic movement to build power. Clinging on harder turns out to be a small part of it. Nea Kunnari, our lead verticals teacher, runs the intermediate silks and open level rope, while Kirsty Arnott teaches open level silks.

Aerial Edge is a not-for-profit circus school in Glasgow, training students from age one to people in their seventies. Whatever your starting point, there's a place for you in the air.

Pick your apparatus below, or try both and find your favourite.
