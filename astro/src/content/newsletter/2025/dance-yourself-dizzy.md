## FLOOR TO FLIGHT

NEW CLASS ADDS CREATIVITY TO YOUR TRAINING

We’ve always known that dance and dance-acro are foundational skills for any circus artist. They build strength, coordination, musicality, and creativity – all things that make your aerials, acro tricks, and partner work look and feel better.

Historically, getting people interested in dance has been a challenge. It can feel intimidating, or like a separate skill to learn. So we're making it more accessible by adding Amber’s new Contemporary Dance for Aerialists to our Circus Fitness family. It’s easier than ever to explore floor work, movement quality, and expressive choreography alongside your aerial and acro training.

I genuinely believe this integration could change how our students approach their circus skills. Dance isn’t just an add-on – it’s a tool for expression, confidence, and creativity in the air and on the floor. I can’t wait to see the ideas, sequences, and performances that emerge when our students embrace it.

See you in class,

Mark Gibson

Aerial Edge Founder

## FRIDAY FINDS ITS RHYTHM

DANCE TO TRANSFORM YOUR AERIAL & ACROBATIC FLOW

Dance and aerial are like that couple everyone roots for – they’re brilliant together, but each is fiercely independent so they often don't talk to each other.

Some people glide easily from one to the other, while others arrive at circus precisely because dance felt too rigid. Aerial gives freedom, dance offers form.

It’s time this became less like a competition and more like collaboration.

Enter Amber Dollin’s new class – Contemporary Dance for Aerialists – the first step towards a beautiful partnership. And here’s the great bit – it’s part of our Circus Fitness family.

Why you’ll love it:

    🪩 Just £7.50 with an eight-class Circus Fitness pass – unbeatable value.

    🪩 One hour long – perfect if you’re unsure or short on time.

    🪩 Completes your Circus Fitness week – five days, five focuses, a full foundation for circus arts.

Amber draws on contemporary techniques and floor work to help you explore creative movement that translates seamlessly into the air. Each session blends technique, skill development, and choreographic play, building spatial awareness, movement quality, and originality.

So if you’ve ever wondered what happens when dance and aerial stop bickering and start loving, here’s your chance to find out – and to finish your week flying.

## ROPE PLAY LIKE NEVER BEFORE

TWISTS ON DROPS AND OTHER DELIGHTS

Step into the rope world like never before with Fox McLavin – aerialist, rope wizard, and self-declared aerial gremlin.

Nearly a decade in the air has shaped their style, but so has their experience as a queer, neurodivergent artist with a real fear of heights. The result is a playful, inventive approach to rope that turns problem-solving into movement that’s all your own.

Gremlin 101 is designed for intermediate to advanced aerialists ready to explore fluidity, twists, creature-like qualities, and sequences that feel good in the body. Expect:

    • Spirals and creative wraps

    • Bold drops and perspective shifts

    • Rope-as-frame experiments and lasso twists

    • Exercises that unleash your inner gremlin

Follow your instincts, get weird, and discover how liberating “gremlin movement” can be.

Prerequisites: hiplock from a scissor kick, solid ankle wraps, flamenco grips, and catchers.

## TWISTS, TURNS, TOGETHER

AERIALISTS MOVE FROM SOLO TO DUO ACT

Students from the Performance Programme are taking their skills in an exciting new direction. As they develop their work for their ensemble show in December, these young aerialists are experimenting with dual silks rigged from a single point, a departure from the solo routines they’ve performed before.

This new challenge encourages them to explore shared movement, timing, and storytelling, finding inventive ways to interact while moving together on the apparatus. Each twist, wrap, and transition sparks creativity, letting them develop distinct characters and narratives on the silks.

Beyond technique, this collaboration builds trust, teamwork, and imagination, helping students push the boundaries of what they thought possible in performance.

Their final show performance piece promises to be a unique glimpse into how young artists can inspire each other when they work together—fluid, dynamic, and full of surprises.
