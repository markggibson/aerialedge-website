GABBY: THE LIGHTS IN OUR LIVES

HIS TECH EFFECT WORK INTRODUCES EXTRA CREATIVITY

Last week’s Open Rig Night was something else, our most impressive tech yet and some of the best-loved acts. Gabby – always the innovator – spent the week developing a new lighting programme that synced with the music, taking the stress out of the show and giving the performers some extra wow-time.

It started as a workaround and became a piece of genius. So much so, a pro lighting engineer in the audience felt compelled to come and ask how the heck he was doing it.

It wasn’t just about looking good. This step change in tech is already shaping how our full-time course students approach their acts, letting them experiment with lighting effects as a creative tool rather than an afterthought.

On Open Rig Night, that careful planning came alive with Huck, one of our one-year Professional Development Course students, whose detailed cues and timing added layers of drama and impact to his performance.

It took a while for the buzz of Open Rig Night to fade, but you have to move on quickly at Aerial Edge. October Edge is here, and we’re already working on Easter Edge 2026 and the Foundation Course ensemble show, among other projects.

For all of us, the circus world is constantly evolving, and there’s always a new edge to reach.

Mark Gibson

Aerial Edge Founder

THE VIEW FROM THE EDGE

WISH YOU WERE HERE? DON'T MISS THE NEXT ONE!

The October Edge has been an absolute buzz from the get-go. The space was alive with new people trying new things, regular students pushing their limits, and instructors bringing something extra to every session.

Guest artist Jason’s workshops were packed – no surprise there. Even his warm-up had people smiling, as he introduced a creative way to connect movement, awareness, and togetherness before diving into his precision straps work.

As ever, people at the fly rig made the most noise. That magical, heart-in-your-throat moment when first-time flyers make it all the way to catch never gets old. It was great to have Hamish back in action alongside Doretto and Jonah. They rotated between lines, platform, catching, and a station with a static bar for technique tutorials.

Wish you were here? That special hum of energy when everyone’s training hard, cheering each other on, and doing things they didn’t think they could? The next chance isn’t far away…

RIDE THE RHYTHM

DYNAMIC HOOP & ROPE WORKSHOPS WITH FREYA

If you’ve ever wished your aerial had a little more whoosh, Freya Pellie is here to deliver. Her dynamic workshops on rope and hoop are all about finding that magic moment when gravity turns into your dance partner.

Freya will show you how to make the trickiest beats feel fun and freeing, helping you discover how to move with power, precision and just the right amount of chaos. Expect big swings, snappy transitions, and that satisfying rush when it all clicks.

Dynamic aerial isn’t just about big beats or flashy drops. It’s about finding rhythm, control, and flow. It's about moving with the rope or hoop.

Freya’s known for her ability to break down complex techniques into clear, achievable progressions, offering both foundations for newcomers to dynamics and spicy variations for those already hooked.

Whether you’re expanding your dynamic vocabulary or just curious what “dynamic” really means, expect new movement pathways, creative transitions, and that addictive feeling when momentum suddenly takes you to a whole new place.

FULL HOUSE, FULL HEARTS

OPEN RIG NIGHT IS THE BEST

The first time is the best, so they say. Maybe it’s the nerves, maybe it’s the rush…but when it’s good, you just want to do it again.

For every performer at Open Rig Night, and perhaps most of all the first-time performers, having a 100-strong audience that really got behind them made it a magical memory. So thank you to everyone who showed up and enthusiastically supported the artists on stage.

It really was a fantastic show, and worth far more than the £10 ticket for two solid hours of top-class circus artistry.

Sam's Night Fever acrobatics with Henry the Hoover was a fan favourite – he was persuaded to create that on the hoof to follow Jamie's rope and silks act which mused on the convergence of office life and an aerial adventures.

Kirsty's blindfolded silks act took courage of one kind, and for performers including Julia and Lucy trying new acts with different types of apparatus, it was a different kind.

And for everyone, it was an absolute triumph. Bring on the next show!
