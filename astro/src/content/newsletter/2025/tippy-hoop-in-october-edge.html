FLYING HIGH, THINKING CLEAR

CIRCUS FUELS YOU FOR LIFE'S CHALLENGES

This week, Aerial Edge packed up its sparkle and set off to Glasgow Freshers’ Week to meet a whole new crowd of students – the kind who normally live among books, essays, and libraries rather than hoops, mats, and trapezes.

We weren’t there to whisk them away from their studies. They’ve only justleft home for uni; running off to join the circus might be a step too far (for now).

We were there to introduce them to a different sort of student life. One that says: get out of your head and into your body. And that applies to all of us.

Swinging, balancing, or tumbling doesn’t just give you a break from work, it fires up the brain, sharpens your focus, and gets the blood (and the laughter) flowing.

It’s something all of us here already know: circus isn’t just play, it’s fuel. Whether you’re crunching numbers at work, tackling a school project, or keeping a family running smoothly, time in the air or on the mat makes the rest of life feel a whole lot lighter.

So, while the freshers got their first taste, let’s all take this as a reminder. We don’t have to run away with the circus – we get to bring the circus back with us, into everything we do.

Mark Gibson

Aerial Edge Founder

Company for aerialists

There’s a nice opportunity for professional aerialists – a long-term contract with Scotland’s first professional aerial ensemble performance company.

All or Nothing Aerial Dance Theatre are looking for four artists to join.

The project has funding to build the company over three years, starting this November.

Jennifer Paterson from All or Nothing says: “Working with artists long term allows us to build the stability and consistency of training together in a positive way.

“People can flourish in that environment. You get better together, it becomes very supportive, you get a sense of each other, you get to know who’s good at what and creativity flows.”

In its pilot year, the four selected aerialists will train, rehearse, and perform together over two blocks: 17th–30th November 2025, and 12th January until 22nd March 2026. The journey continues into 2026–27, with longer contracts and more performance opportunities to follow.

While the company is based in Edinburgh, rehearsals will take place across central Scotland.

Deadline for applications: 5pm on Friday 19th September

Audition: Sunday 28th September 2025, 12:00–17:00 in Dunblane

Apply by sending your CV, showreel link, and artist statement to recruitment@aerialdance.co.uk.

You know you can hang, swing, and spin in aerial hammock at the October Edge but now we can reveal a new twist – Kerri will also be teaching tippy hoop at this new circus festival!

Kerri's workshops were sell-out sensations, so if you’ve been waiting for your chance to give tippy hoop or hammock a whirl, this is it! With three workshops three days in a row, you're giving yourself a great chance for appreciable results.

For the uninitiated, tippy hoop is a deliciously wobbly, tilty, “am-I-flying-or-am-I-falling?” kind of adventure that makes even the most confident aerialists laugh out loud.

Imagine a hoop with a mind of its own – and you learning to dance with it instead of against it. That’s the fun of it, and Kerri makes the learning curve feel like play.

And October Edge is the perfect place to dive in. Over three glorious days, you’ll get three full workshops in your chosen disciplines. That's enough time to wobble, wobble some more...then whoosh! You're flying!

Plus, it’s the best circus bargain around, because we believe circus should be as affordable as it is addictive.

So whether you’re coming for the hammock, the tippy hoop, or simply the thrill of three days surrounded by circus magic, October Edge is where progress happens – and where fun is guaranteed.

PLAY, PERFORM, PROGRESS

COURAGE COUNTS MORE THAN PERFECTION

Think performing is just for the pros? Think again. Open Rig Night is for everyone – youth, adults in recreational classes, full-time circus students, instructors, and professionals alike.

And yes, your act can be a masterpiece in progress (half-baked ideas are surprisingly good under the lights).

So why take the leap? Here are a few reasons:

  • • You’ll never forget your first applause. The butterflies might flutter, but they usually turn into fireworks once you’re up there.

  • • It makes your training real. Tricks in class are great but sharing them with an audience gives you extra sparkle.

  • • Work-in-progress is welcome. Nobody expects perfect. We applaud creativity, courage, and a wobbly landing or two.

  • • The audience is on your side. It’s your circus family out there. They’ll cheer whether you nail it or noodle it.

  • • Performing is the best teacher. Each time you step into the spotlight, you grow not just as a performer, but as a person.

So, dust off that hoop, silks, trapeze – or whatever wild idea you’ve been brewing in the back of your mind. Sign up now, and let Open Rig Night be the next step in your circus adventure.

Because the truth is: the act is never just about the tricks, it’s about the thrill of daring to share them.

GREMLIN 101

NEW ROPE APPROACH WITH FOX

Gremlin 101 is a one-off rope workshop with Fox Mclavin, a self-described aerial gremlin whose movement style has been informed by their lived experiences as a queer neurodivergant person as well as their fear of heights.

In nearly nine years of aerial, Fox has gotten incredibly creative about managing his fear of heights whilst in the air. This has led to the development of some truly weird and wonderful shapes - which you will explore in this workshop, along with wraps that can support you in making your own twists.

You'll look at sequences that ascend the apparatus, a drop leading into a unique approach to the rope as a frame as well as some unique approaches to lassoos.

This workshop is ideal for intermediate-advanced rope enthusiasts.

Pre requisites: hiplock from scissor kick, comfortable with ankle wraps, flamenco grips, catchers.

BALANCING PACTS

WHEN SHARING TURNS INTO AERIAL MAGIC

In life, there’s usually only one rule when kids play: take turns. But these are circus kids! This group of young acrobats didn't get the memo – or they just it demonstrated that there can be a better way.

Instead of battling it out for their moment in the spotlight, three brilliant minds came together to form the Human Hoop Tower! That set them off on course for creating a trio act.

Witnesses reported zero squabbles, minimal wobbling, and maximum enjoyment. Rumour has it, Cirque du Soleil might already be drafting contracts.

Until then, we’ll just call it teamwork, circus-style: a mix of creativity, courage, and just a magical amount of chaos.
