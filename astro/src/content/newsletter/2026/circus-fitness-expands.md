## FRIENDS AT THE FRINGE

## OMAR AND THE PALESTINIAN CIRCUS BRING STEP AND A HALF

The Palestinian Circus: Step and a Half | The Palestinian Circus, with Medical Aid for Palestinians

There's a moment in a Levantine folk dance called Dabkeh where a whole line of dancers stamps the floor at the same moment. It lands like a visible heartbeat of The Palestinian Circus at the Fringe, in a show where traditional Arab dance and music meet contemporary circus in its diverse glory.

Dabkeh carries real weight for Palestinians specifically, and this show puts that same energy into the air too, moving that rhythmic, floor-bound power into dance trapeze, silks, straps, partner acro, flow, fire spinning and more.

That's the pull of Step and a Half: Palestinian folk dance and serious circus skill sharing one stage, sometimes in the same breath, moving between grounded and airborne.

One of the people making all of that happen is Omar, a friend of Yara's. That this company exists at all, and has made it to a stage in Edinburgh, is awe-inspiring. That they have produced a show which is both uplifting and touching as well as hugely impressive is a triumph that deserves recognition.

They've produced this despite adversity of the sort that most of us cannot even imagine, and they're going back to face more of the same after the Fringe.

Bringing a fantastic energy and a boisterous tenderness to The Meadows, these guys are worth a visit for sure.

Book it for the skill, the dance, or just for the chance to watch a friend of ours do it in front of a room that doesn't know his name yet.

## BE BAD AT SOMETHING NEW

## THEN SPEND THE WEEKEND GETTING BETTER AT IT

There's a moment that October Edge booking sometimes involves: you frown at the timetable and realise you can't make the options work into your ideal configuration.

Some of the tracks fall into place – arts you love, and you would happily spend the whole weekend worshipping at that altar. Some are interesting alternatives that hold some appeal to you. But you're left with one time slot that gives you the fear, or just has nothing you fancy.

Go Nike: Just Do It.

It's that simple. Go ahead and be bad at it, and get better at it. Whatever you pick, it will add value to your weekend, whether that may be in finding transferrable skills, unexpected laughs, or the satisfaction of feeling the fear and doing it anyway.

Here's what NOT to do:

- Be worried that people will judge you. Somewhere inside, you know the people in this place aren't like that. God knows, we've told you often enough! We all begin somewhere, and we remember that.

- Be worried that you can't do it. If you can't do it on the first day, accept that there will be a bit less can't and a bit more can on the second day. That's how practice works. Small, incremental changes are valid.

- Be a perfectionist. Tell yourself whatever you need to, to get yourself to a place where you book your ticket despite the fact that it's not falling precisely as you want it. Don't cut off your nose to spite your face, as your granny wisely told you!

This week we're tempting you into a ticket by revealing that Sam will be there to teach trampoline, acro and possibly handstands too. Hands up who's in for that? Or hands down?!

At risk of being repetitive, you don't need to already be good at trampoline or tumbling to book Sam's tracks. What you need is three days built around being new at something.

And who knows? The thing you weren't sure about on Friday might be the thing you're doing again the week after the Edge, just because you can.

## THE CATCH STARTS ON THE GROUND

## NEW CLASS: CIRCUS FITNESS FOR FLYING TRAPEZE

The second you let go of the flying trapeze bar can now be set up for success somewhere else entirely. Like on a trampoline, or a static bar or without ever leaving the ground.

That's the idea behind Tuesday's new class, Circus Fitness: Flying Trapeze.

You'll be taught by both Gabe and Mark, moving from trampoline-based air awareness, building up to full rotations and twists, into swing work with conditioning specific to fly.

What? You thought there wouldn't be a kill and build section? Don't be daft.

You can already guess that's going to involve leg lifts and pull-ups, and there will also be exercises to work on the kind of body tension that improves your flight.

We're not saying it'll be easy, but we can guarantee results!

## REMEMBER YOUR FIRST DAY?

## NOW IMAGINE DOING IT AGAIN WITH AN AUDIENCE WATCHING

You remember your first day in your first circus class? It probably felt enormous, your body doing something it had no business doing yet, and still deciding whether or not it could grapple with gravity and win.

Then you learned one trick. Then another. At some point, without you necessarily noticing when, the impossible thing became the thing you do most Tuesdays.

Open Rig Night is one of those moments, just moved to a stage. If the thought of doing it in front of people makes your stomach perform a small backflip of its own, that's a completely normal thing to feel, and it doesn't mean you're not ready.

Performance isn't compulsory, unless you're on a full-time course – it would be a sad kind of madness to miss the Open Rig Night opportunity when your next step is an ensemble show.

But if any part of you has been wondering if you might be able to perform something, if there's a small wish in your heart that you could one day do it, Open Rig Night is what that's for.

You've already done the hard part, getting on the rig the first time, when you had no idea what you were doing.

Talk to your instructor, or to Mark or Yara. They'll help you work out what you've actually got, or are close to getting, and how to get it in front of an audience.
