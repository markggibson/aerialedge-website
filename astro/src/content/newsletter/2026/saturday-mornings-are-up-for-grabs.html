SATURDAY MORNINGS ARE UP FOR GRABS

FIT TO FLY IS SORTED, THE REST IS YOURS TO NAME

Wherever we've been based, Saturday mornings have wanted flying trapeze on them. It filled up when we were at Kelvin Hall, and before that at the Briggait, so it's time to try it here too.

What's new about fly when it starts this September is the hour before it. I've taught Straps and Aerial Conditioning for years (currently Mondays at 7pm), and it's better than Tinkerbell's fairy dust for making you fly.

Fit to Fly puts that pairing on a Saturday: conditioning at 9am, flying trapeze at 10am, with the same instructor monitoring your progress.

Seeing as the space is open, we might as well do other things on a Saturday morning, if you're up for it. There may be nothing more likely to induce an eye roll than that hollow assurance 'we value your feedback' from a corporate giant, but not among friends. Here, we thrive on it! Your feedback changes what you get.

A hunch tells us what might work. Build it and they will come, it is said, so we do that. But if you tell us you will come, we will build it. Youth CIrcus Saturdays? Family circus Saturdays? Your favourite aerial or acro class?

Email info@contact.aerialedge.co.uk and tell us what would actually get you here on a Saturday morning, regularly, and that's what we'll build toward next.

Mark Gibson
Aerial Edge Founder

THE SKY BEHAVED ITSELF

UNITY IN DIVERSITY OUTLASTED A STORM WARNING

For a few hours last Friday, every forecast app in Glasgow was calling for thunder and lightning. Not brilliant news for a show built around a metal rig standing in the middle of a park.

Aerial Edge's Unity in Diversity went ahead, at Kelvingrove Park's Performance Zone, as part of the Commonwealth Games Festival programme. Yara, Clara, Pegg and Elke each carried one of the show's four elements – air, fire, earth and metal – across silks, hoop, duo rope and flying pole, dipping in and out of the air together rather than each staying in their own lane.

As it turned out, the rain dried and the rig wasn't at risk of becoming a lightning conductor. What showed up instead was a crowd that reacted in harmony with the Elements on stage, loving every minute of it, storm warning forgotten.

Unity in Diversity was a one-off – and Open Rig Night in October is full of those. That's your next chance to catch a stage full of people doing what they love for your entertainment and delight – and this time it's indoors, where you provide the thunderous applause.

 

Aerial Edge presented Unity in Diversity
Friday 31st July | 6.30pm-7pm
Kelvingrove Park, Performance Zone, Commonwealth Games Festival programme

NOBODY HERE IS JUST WATCHING

OPEN RIG NIGHT RETURNS THIS OCTOBER FOR PROS AND FIRST-TIMERS ALIKE

For one night in October, the rig steps up from being your support to become your stage, and the audience then assumes the role of your support.

Open Rig Night is built on blur. People can find themselves on stage having arrived at Aerial Edge for the first time as an audience member, a student trying circus for the first time with nothing but nerve to build on, or as a pro looking for a training space. Somewhere along the way, they created an act that they wanted to perform to a supportive audience. It's often unclear at what point the urge took hold.

Full-time students don't have much choice, Open Rig Night the first performance opportunity on their course and they're always ready, whether they believe it or not. Recreational students need to be encouraged and the belief that they're capable usually needs to have been generated if not fully flourished before they step on stage. Youth Circus students who are keen (turns out there are plenty) tend to relish their time to shine. And pros sometimes need to see how an act that they are developing feels in front of a crowd.

Whatever the performer, the room is on your side, whether it's seconds into your first trick or a hundred rehearsals deep into one you've been chasing all year. That's one of the characteristics of Open Rig Night.

The other is that it tends to fill up and sell out. Want a slot? Start putting an act together now. Want a seat? The same clock is ticking.

 

Open Rig Night
Saturday 10th October | 6.30pm-9pm
Tickets £10 / £5

FOCUS OVER FITNESS

REAP THE REWARDS OF STICKING WITH IT THREE DAYS STRAIGHT

Ninety minutes with any discipline is a decent learning curve. What if you didn't then have a week for the memory to fade before you're back trying to remember where you left off?

October Edge runs on the rhythm of focus more than fitness. Sure, you'll be tired, but you won't notice nearly as much as you think, because you'll be loving how you're elevating yourself in every way.

Pick your four tracks – your favourite arts and/or whatever's been sitting on your someday list – and you'll in them for one session each, three days running. Muscle memory doesn't fade in between sessions, quite the reverse, it gains momentum. Saturday morning picks up where Friday afternoon left off, and Sunday starts from wherever Saturday got you to.

Beginners and old hands train in the same room either way, each at their own pace, everyone supported by their instructors and each other.

And rest assured, flying trapeze will be on all day every day. Although your hands would be shredded if you did literally just that for three days straight.

Cost £200

THE ONLY ONE WHO GETS IT?

EXCHANGE CIRCUS IDEAS WITH OTHERS LIKE YOU

If you're raising the only kid in the friend group who knows what a wrap is, never mind a drop, the Scottish Youth Circus Exchange is worth knowing about.

That's the gap the Exchange is built to close. On Saturday 22nd August, Aerial Art House and All or Nothing are running the second one at the Dunblane Centre, with support and funding from the National Centre for Circus. SAME DIAMETER, OPPOSITE JOBS

WHY TWO ROPES THAT LOOK THE SAME ARE RATED BY DIFFERENT METHODS

Pick up two 11mm ropes, same fibre, same kernmantle build, and read what's printed on them. One quotes the force it delivers and a number of falls held. The other quotes a breaking strength and a stretch percentage. Same diameter, and not one shared number between them.

This week's article on circusrigging.info explains why. A dynamic climbing rope exists to catch a fall, so its standard rates it on the force it passes into a falling body, and never publishes a breaking load at all. A semi-static rope exists to hold a rigger steady, so its standard rates it on raw strength and how little it stretches. Opposite jobs, opposite tests, and the diameter is the one thing that can't tell you which rope you're holding.

There's history in there too: hemp lines that were expected to snap, wartime parachute cord, and how each standard ended up asking its own question.

Six minutes of reading, and you'll never size up a rope by its diameter again.

FOUR SHOWS, ONE CIRCUS EXPERT OPINION

VEE'S PICKS FOR WHAT'S WORTH YOUR FRINGE MONEY THIS AUGUST

The Fringe throws a few thousand shows at you every August and expects you to figure out which ones are worth the ticket. Vee's doing some of that work for you. Four picks, four different moods, all circus in one way or another. More picks next week.

LOCAL TALENT

Tell Me | Sadiq Ali Company

Part of the Made in Scotland Showcase, and featuring Aerial Edge's own Jonah on Chinese pole, partner acro and aerial cube. Tell Me is a circus-dance show where past and present realities of living with HIV collide. It previewed at Manipulate Festival Edinburgh earlier this year to multiple five-star reviews.

7pm at Summerhall, Dissection Room, running 6th until 31st August (not Mondays or Tuesdays), 1hr 5min, age 14+. Book here

 

The Raft of the Crab | Ninon Noiret

Over 10 years in the making, this is Ninon's own true story of cancer recovery as a young woman, told through spoken word, song, Chinese pole, dance acro and puppetry. Powerful.

2.30pm at Assembly Roxy, Central, running 18th until 31st August (not 24th or 25th), 1hr, age 16+. Book here

 

FOR EVERYONE

Ten Thousand Hours | Gravity and Other Myths (Australia)

GOM's third consecutive year at the Fringe, and an ensemble acrobatic show about mastery, skill, play, failure, trying again, and succeeding – and, most importantly, doing really cool acro tricks! Dozens of five-star reviews already, and astonished audience members will tell you that this is truly a show that needs to be seen to be believed.

5.30pm at Assembly Hall, Main Hall, running 6th until 30th August (not 12th, 17th or 24th), 1hr, age 5+. Book here

 

ADULTS ONLY

Bernie Dieter's Club Kabarett | Bernie Dieter

When Cabaret is spelled with a K you know you're in for a good night! International touring icon Bernie Dieter returns to Edinburgh with her new offering: Club Kabarett promising sultry circus, dangerous songs, and a riotous atmosphere to rival the original Kit Kat Club. Expect aerial, fire, contortion and more.

7.20pm at Underbelly Circus Hub, The Meadows, running 7th until 29th August (not 17th or 24th), 1hr 15min, 16+. Book here

 

FEELING ARTY?

DUSK/NIGHT/DAWN | Vertical Dreamscapes

Returning to Edinburgh after their cult break-out hit last year, this company treads the line between performance art, circus and surrealist dance. USA Pole Art Champion Donna Carnow leads a string of striking, visually led dance vignettes that touch on womanhood, the lure of the ingénue, the ridiculousness of beauty standards, and what it means to dream. Featuring some of the most astonishing solo and duo fly pole you are likely to see anywhere.

9.30pm at Assembly Roxy, Central, running 5th until 30th August, 1hr, 16+. Book here
