## YOU DON’T OUTGROW CIRCUS

## YOU JUST KEEP DISCOVERING WHAT YOU CAN DO NEXT

One of the things we love most about circus is that there is no finish line.

Just when you think you've mastered something, you discover a new variation, a new challenge, or a completely different way of approaching it. The goalposts keep moving – not because you're failing to reach them, but because you're growing.

We see students all the time who are convinced they can't climb that rope, hold that handstand, learn that sequence, or perform in front of an audience.

Yet little by little, they do it. Not because they suddenly become fearless, most often because they keep showing up.

And that's the real magic of circus. The tricks themselves as cool of course, but so is the process of becoming someone who can do things they once thought impossible.

So if you’re considering joining us now after being inspired by our Commonwealth Games Festival events or Moira’s story in the media, just know that you don’t have to be ready for the whole journey. Just the next step.

The regulars here will back me up in saying it’s the start of a great adventure.

## WHO'S  HOOP

## HOW YOU TRAIN CHANGES HOW YOU FEEL

To the uninitiated, aerial hoop may seem like a question of spins, shapes, strength and control. But where it takes you can look very different depending on the class you step into.

At Aerial Edge, there isn’t a single path through hoop, we have three distinct instructors, plus hoop features in mixed aerial classes too. None is better than the others. They just suit different people in different ways.

Some people find a class and stick with it. It feels like home. Others like to switch, to challenge themselves, try different coaching styles, and see what else opens up. And some end up somewhere in between.

If you’re new to aerial, hoop appears in our Aerial Absolute Beginners class on Mondays, and Sunday Mixed Aerial – these give you a chance to explore, experiment and get comfortable in the air.

In the three dedicated hoop classes each week, Clara, Kerri and Freya P each brings their own approach.

## Clara’s Beginner Level Monday's 7pm-8.30pm

Clara focuses on building strong foundations through conditioning, fundamental skills and shapes, and learning how to link movements into flowing sequences.

## Kerri’s Open Level Tuesdays 7pm-8.30pm

Kerri designs this for those who have aerial hoop basics and are ready to deepen their practice. Each week she brings a fresh combination of spins, dynamics and flowing sequences, with progressions and regressions so you can work at your own level.

## Freya’s Open Level Thursdays 7pm-8.30pm

Freya combines conditioning, dynamics and choreographic sequences, with a structured but adaptable approach that supports both beginners/improvers and more advanced students working on progression.

The point isn’t to try to find the “right” class.

It’s to find the one that makes you want to come back, and to play with what hoop feels like for you.

## WHISPERS OF GOLD

## DIRECTOR’S INSIGHTS FROM FOUNDATION COURSE SHOW

Have you ever heard a whisper in the dead of night? Or felt a strange sense of being watched as you walked a lonely path?

When you see Whispers of Old, that sense of unease is where everything begins. And for that to be what you feel from your seat in the audience, a whole world of folklore and mythical creatures had to be created and embodied by the students who star in the show.

For director Michelle Ross, the ideas form and flow by breaking that world into tasks.

She explains: “I work in a devising capacity. I create tasks for the students then they create their work from those tasks – they put in their own tricks, their own personality based on the task.”

Those tasks have been rooted in the mythology – encounters, portals, transformations, moments of temptation or warning – and the students respond through aerial, acrobatics and movement, but also through their own creative instincts and storytelling ideas. Characters begin to emerge through their interpretations, rather than being pre-set on the page.

As material develops, Michelle shapes and refines it. She decides how these mythic fragments connect, how each creature or encounter sits within the wider journey, and how the world of the piece expands across the ensemble.

What appears on stage is therefore both guided and discovered. It’s a combination of structured storytelling and student-generated movement drawn from the themes of Celtic mythological creatures like selkies and kelpies, rivers and lochs that seem to beckon, distant lights that might offer safety…or something else entirely.

The result is a show built from the uncertainty of encounters between story and body, myth and imagination, task and response.

It’s a world of whispers, made by listening closely to them.

## YOUR BEST DANCE PARTNER EVER

## YOU’LL LOVE FLY POLE EVEN IF IT TRIES TO FLOOR YOU

Everyone who’s anyone in fly pole around these parts is buzzed that Elke is back with a full day of spinny, dynamic, slightly unhinged (in the best way) aerial goodness.

Whether you’re chasing big dynamic tricks, smoother transitions, or that addictive feeling of spinning through space while still trying to stay in control, there’s a workshop designed to meet you there.

Across the day, Elke is offering level-based sessions as well as a dedicated choreography workshop for anyone who loves the more creative side of fly pole. This is where the apparatus becomes less about individual tricks and more about flow. You’ll start from the floor and build into movement, exploring rhythm, musicality, transitions, and how to make pole feel like a partner in the dance, even when it’s trying to fling you off.

The choreography session is open and adaptable, designed for beginner to intermediate movers with options to simplify or extend depending on experience.

Alongside this, the levelled workshops give space to either push into more advanced work or stay where you are and refine it. Returning students can level up if they’re ready, while others can revisit familiar material with new variations and extra challenge options built in.

Elke’s approach is simple: meet people where they are, then make them slightly braver than they expected.
Basically, there’s a workshop with your name on it.
