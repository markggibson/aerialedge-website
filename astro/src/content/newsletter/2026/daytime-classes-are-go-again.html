THE SURPRISING WINTER WARM-UP

YOU, CLASSES AND A COSY ROOM

The nights are fair drawing in, as the nation's grannies love to say. Which means that while people may not be shivering yet, pretty soon nobody's going to be quick to take their coat off either.

We're looking for a better way to heat the place – something that actually works, doesn't cost a fortune to run, and doesn't make the planet wince every time we turn it on.

Green energy may yet turn out to be our friend, but in the meantime, the most effective heating system is actually a room full of circus people. Not kidding. It's appreciably warmer when more people are training, we've been in this building long enough to notice.

It's also the tidiest kind of arrangement. More people through the door means more warmth in the room, and more income to spend on heating it when there aren't so many people around. Enough of that, and the thermostat might actually get to a place that feels indulgently cosy.

So if you've been considering adding another class to your schedule, or bringing a friend, you'll be doing yourself and all of us a favour this winter.

Eventually, if enough of us do it, we all get the sort of life we deserve.

Mark Gibson
Aerial Edge Founder

CAP ON OPEN RIG NIGHT ACTS – REGISTER SOON TO BE ON STAGE

For years, Open Rig Night has operated on a fairly simple principle: if someone wants to perform, we'll find them somewhere to do it.

There may be a physical limit to this theory. At some point, the audience would like to go home. The Subway would like its passengers back. And the people running the evening would quite like to know what time they are finishing.

It's a measure of student success that the numbers have been growing both on stage and in the audience. So for the first time, we'll have to cap the number of acts.

There are five adult performer slots left, and three for Youth Circus. First people to register get on the bill.

The support we can give you won't change. If you've got an idea but aren't sure whether it's ready, talk to your instructor, Mark or Yara. We'll help you work out what it needs.

And if you'd rather be on the other side of the lights, tickets are on sale too. The last two Open Rig Nights sold out, so this is probably not the evening to practise your ability to make decisions at the last possible moment.

EDGE SCHEDULE REVEALED

EVERYTHING INCLUDING WARM-UP AND COOL-DOWN NOW LOCKED IN

Here it is...the full October Edge schedule. In between warm up and cool down sessions, there are enough circus disciplines to send anyone into a spin of excited indecision. It's a child in a sweetie shop moment.

The first time slot choices are flying trapeze with Hamish and Kirsty, rope with Nea, tippy hoop with Kerri, or acrobatics with Sam. Pick one and settle in for three days of visible progress.

After the break, flying trapeze keeps the same pair, while Robyn takes straps and loops, Michelle brings Chinese pole and Kerri gives you aerial hammock.

Straps and loops comes with a little bit of circus history. The swinging version we teach today grew out of a static circus trick, developed into something much more airborne by twin brothers Yuri and Valery Panteleenko in Soviet circus in the early 1970s. Pick it, and you're in on a slightly obscure bit of circus history too.

Then comes lunch, after which flying trapeze changes shape. Gabe, Hamish and Nea take over, with cradle work in the mix. In the same time slot, Helen has fly pole, Kirsty has silks and Sam has handstands.

The final stretch brings Gabe and Kirsty back to flying trapeze, Sam on trampoline, Robyn on partner and group acrobatics, and Mark with doubles trapeze.

The bit that makes October and Easter Edge work is that whatever four arts you choose for Friday are the same four arts you work on every day of the festival.

You have three days getting properly into the four things you've picked, finding the awkward bits, trying them again, discovering they aren't quite so impossible any more, and doing them again because now you want to.

All of which happens in a fun-fuelled atmosphere full of people in the same boat regardless of their level of expertise – trying to nail moves that matter to them.

BUSY DAYS AND NIGHT IN THE SPOTLIGHT

OPEN RIG NIGHT THEN HALF-TERM CAMP

Sometime in October you walk into Aerial Edge in the daytime and realise the shoes by the door have got considerably smaller.

Half-term has arrived.

From Monday 12th to Friday 16th, Youth Circus Camp takes over from 10am-4pm, with five days of aerial, acro, flying trapeze and whatever else gets thrown into the mix. The rig gets busy, the mats get busy, and the volume gets considerably louder.

You can come for a single day or stay for the whole week – £60 for a day, £240 for all five. That's one full day free.

Friday afternoon ends with the bit everyone secretly likes best: the final half hour opens up to family and friends, so the students can show off what they've been learning while they've been out the house all week.

Showing off isn't just socially acceptable at Aerial Edge, it's actively encouraged.

Open Rig Night on Saturday 10th opens with Youth Circus students. If your young performer wants to get on the bill, they just need to speak to Nea in class. But do it quick – there are only three spots left.

And whether they're performing or watching, family and friends are very much encouraged to make an unreasonable amount of noise.
