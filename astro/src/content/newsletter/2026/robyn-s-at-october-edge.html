FOUR WEEKS THAT DON'T NEED TO LEAD ANYWHERE

IT'S MORE THAN JUST WHAT YOU CAN DO

We often talk about the Four-Week Intensive Course as a first step. Do the four weeks, see how it feels, carry on to the Foundation Course if it grabs you.

But that framing can sell it short. It's more than an audition for something bigger, it's very much worth doing on its own. Training all day, every weekday for a month changes how you move, what you can achieve, and how you feel about all. It stays changed, too.

This Friday the current course finishes and one student will go on the Foundation Course, while others go back to their jobs and their families and their weekly classes, carrying that month with them. It brings a new sense of what's possible.

The next course starts 28 September. A month of training is more than it sounds.

Mark Gibson
Aerial Edge Founder

It sits second in the running order of the day's workshops, from 11.30am, which puts it right after Advanced and just before Intermediate. Worth configuring your day around it!

It's a pacy conditioning class that covers aerial and acro needs, including some emphasis on core strength.

THREE HOURS, ONE MAT, NO SYLLABUS

ACRO JAM IS NEXT SATURDAY, JULIA AND ROBYN HOSTING

Three hours is a long time to be on a mat with no lesson plan...and that's the beauty of Acro Jam.

It's a different proposition from a class. A 90-minute class has to get somewhere. Acro Jam runs 12.30pm-3.30pm and gets wherever the room takes it.

You have time to fail at a lift 11 times and land it on the 12th, which is roughly how many attempts a new lift actually takes. You also have time to stand about and watch, which is fun in itself as well as educational in its own way.

Julia and Robyn open the doors, pull the lines and keep an eye on things. Bring a partner, or turn up on your own and find some people to play with.

ARE YOU UP FOUR IT?

THE NEXT INTENSIVE OPENS AT THE END OF SEPTEMBER

The Four-Week Intensive is what you do when you want to find out what full-time training feels like without signing away months to get the answer. Or when you need a meaningful break from your day job, without quitting.

You might come in with one apparatus in mind and spend the first fortnight discovering that you are falling in love with something else. That opens your options for what you will perform at the showing of your work on the last afternoon of your course, which friends and family can come watch.

By the end of the month, you have a body that works differently and a clearer idea of what you want to do next. It might be the Foundation Course, or going back to your old life with new perspective and confidence.

The current cohort finishes this Friday – a moment of sadness, pride, and maybe decision-making about how life after circus changes. The next course starts Monday 28 September and runs til Friday 23 October.

YOU ALREADY KNOW HALF THE CAST

OPEN RIG NIGHT LETS YOU SEE THEM IN A DIFFERENT LIGHT

The person who's sometimes on the hoop next to you, the one working the same silks drop every week, the people you say hello to on the way through reception...they might be performing at Open Rig Night.

On Saturday 10th October, some of them walk out under the lights to do the thing they've been practising, but this time in front of 100 or so people.

At Open Rig Night, somebody's first ever act could be followed by somebody's 20th – it's an eclectic and electric mix of people, arts, emotions and excitement.

You'll recognise faces, and you might watch someone you thought you knew do something that reframes them entirely, which is one of the joys of a circus community performance rather than a touring cast you'll never see again.

Since the last two Open Rig Nights sold out, it's wise to book before they all go. Tickets are £10, or £5 for children and concessions, so it's a great value family night out.

And if you have a feeling that watching will no longer satisfy your inner circus artist, registration for acts is open until Friday 3rd October. There's till plenty of time to get your act together.

TURNS OUT YOU CAN BUY HAPPINESS

THREE DAYS AT THE EDGE IS YOURS FOR THE TAKING...FOR NOW

There's a stretch of autumn where nothing much is asked of you, roughly from now until Jingle Bells becomes the irritating backdrop to every supermarket sweep. Summer's done. The Fringe is a memory. The nights are fair drawing in and there are sirens on your sofa luring you into the snuggly depths.

Save yourself! Book October Edge to give yourself a powerful reason to close your ears to the sofa sirens' seductive songs, and steer away from them.

October Edge is three days over the weekend of the 23rd, built so you can pick four arts and stick with them for three days straight. We switched it from Halloween weekend so that those of you who are forcibly or willingly committed to guising can do both.

You don't have to choose a grand goal, you can find your happiness in tiny tickable achievable targets. It could be a skill you've been toying with, an apparatus you keep thinking maybe you'd like to try, a shape that's never quite worked. Pick the thing, give it the weekend, see where it gets to.

You choose four workshops a day and do them every day, so something that felt impossible on Friday is merely difficult by Sunday.

Now, some may say it's hard to pick the goal when you don't know all the arts that are going to be in the Edge. True, they haven't all been announced yet, so here's the latest reveal: Robyn will teach group and partner acro.

This discipline has been gaining popularity at Aerial Edge since Robyn and Julia specialised in it during their Foundation Course and Professional Development Course. Their acts in the shows were always fan favourites, and they've been leading the instruction in the Monday class they launched.

Even if you've never tried group or partner acro, you'll make satisfying progress over the three days of October Edge with Robyn, who is just finishing performing at the Fringe.

The tracks will be open for booking two weeks before the event, and before that, you'll know exactly what's on the schedule.

Whether you're a gambler who will wait for the full line-up and potentially lose your spot is up to you – there are only 36 tickets for this event, and nearly half have been sold.

Waiting for the timetable before you buy your weekend of happiness is the thing most likely to cost you a place.

What we know so far – flying trapeze will be in every time slot, Sam is teaching trampoline and acrobatics, and Kerri will take tippy hoop and hammock.

The sofa will still be there on Sunday evening, and you'll have earned the right to sink into it with as many snacks as you can carry.

REFINE YOUR FLIGHT PATH

WHAT FLYING TRAPEZE IS ACTUALLY BUILT ON

The bits of flying trapeze that decide whether a trick works — the shape in the air, the timing of the release, holding your body tight through a rotation — mostly get built somewhere other than the flying trapeze.

From Tuesday 8th September, that's a class. Circus Fitness: Flying Trapeze, 6pm-7pm, taught by Gabe and Mark together.

You play on the trampoline, building air awareness, then work up to full rotations and twists, then into swing work. There's a kill and build section. And there's conditioning aimed squarely at fly: leg lifts, pull-ups, and the specific kind of body tension that makes you lighter to catch and more likely to make it there.

It won't be a gentle hour. But it will be excellent value.

If you can keep yourself amused through the 90-minute gap that follows (rope, hoop or Chinese pole?), Nea's Fly Fundamentals picks up at 8.30pm the same evening.

Have you spotted the pattern? We're giving you options to refine your flying technique in all sorts of ways, so that you get even more out of a flying trapeze class.

Tuesdays 6pm-7pm from 8th September | Ages 16+
£7.50 on an 8-class pass or £10 drop-in
