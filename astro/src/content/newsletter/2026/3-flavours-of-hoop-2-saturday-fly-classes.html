IT'S RARELY THE BIG DEAL FIRST

QUESTIONS THAT HIDE THE REAL ONE

Nine days from now, a room full of people who've never trained together will turn up on a Monday morning, working out where to park their lunch.

Somebody might ask whether they're allowed to bring their own chalk (yes, you can) and somebody else wants to know where to leave their bag. It's very unlikely that anyone asks about the four weeks of straps, trapeze and acrobatics waiting for them — those questions arise later.

There's a version of nervousness that hides in small, sensible-sounding questions rather than the big ones lurking underneath them. It's not unique to circus. It just happens to be easier to spot in a room where the big question is "can I really do this for four weeks straight?" and the small one is about chalk.

By the time this lot leave, most of them won't remember worrying about it at all, they'll have a bunch of new skills and confidence, knowing that they can do a lot of things that they didn't think they could. And that's what's magical about circus.

Mark Gibson
Aerial Edge Founder

TAKE OFF ON SATURDAYS

TWO CLASSES NOW - THAT TAKES IT TO 10 A WEEK

There are so many take-offs and landings in Aerial Edge airspace these days, we might need to hire an air traffic controller. We've just added yet another flying trapeze class on Saturday mornings, taking the total per week to 10.

And that's not counting the Circus Fitness classes that engineer your aerodynamics to elite level.

You can look at this as more classes to choose from, or more classes to pack into your weekly schedule of activities. You could do something seven days a week, although good practice says you ought to have a day off, but for the obsessive flyers, you could alternate that from week to week so that you're still getting the full range of training options.

Plenty of flyers love back-to-back classes so now that's available on Saturdays, Sundays, Wednesdays and Fridays. All Open Level skills classes. Plus there's Beginners on Mondays and Fly Fundamentals on Tuesdays.

For the groundwork, you have three Circus Fitness options: Wednesdays are dedicated to fly-specific aerial proprioception and conditioning with Mark and Gabe, while Tuesday mornings and Thursday evenings are Yara's aerial conditioning classes.

On top of all this, daytime sessions are bookable some weeks, so it's worth checking the schedule and Instagram regularly.

Still no air traffic controller. Just a very busy kettle.

3 FLAVOURS OF HOOP

CLARA, KERRI AND FREYA EACH TEACH IT DIFFERENTLY

Ask three hoop teachers what their class is about and you'll get three different answers. On purpose.

Clara builds beginners from the ground up on Monday nights – foundations, shapes, linking them into flowing sequences. Kerri takes Open Level on Tuesdays a step past that, working with the skills you've already got until they start looking like your own style. Depending on who's in Thursday's class, Freya usually splits the room in two, beginners and improvers working one way, advanced and pro-track students another, each headed towards their own goals.

WHAT ARE YOU GOOD AT?

FIND OUT WITH A DAY JOB LIKE NO OTHER

The hours of our full-time courses look ordinary enough on paper: 10am to 5pm, Monday to Friday, much like a job. In four weeks of it, what happens inside those hours stops looking or feeling anything like any job you've ever had.

Whether it's four weeks, four months or six months, these days are filled edge-to-edge from the start, with things to make you think and move in ways you haven't done before.

On the final afternoon of the Four-Week Intensive, when family and friends are invited to watch an informal showing of your work to date, no one leaves unimpressed. Including you. You couldn't have done it a month earlier.

By the end of the four-month Foundation Course, you will have performed in Open Rig Night and an ensemble show with a professional director. And by the end of the six-month Professional Development Course, you will have honed your performance skills and starred in a gorgeously lit and staged showreel shot by Gabriel Stella.

None of it happens in isolation, either. It's the same faces throughout your course, working the same daily schedule, so you find strength and support in each other.

Doesn't matter if you're 16 and this is the first course you've ever done, or circus arts have been home ground for years — everyone starts that first Monday the same distance from their final afternoon.

You don't need to know yet what you'll turn out to be good at. If you sign up for the next Intensive, nine days from now, you'll start finding out.

SHOW YOUR CIRCUS SELF

CURIOUS ABOUT WHAT HAPPENS NEXT? PERFORMANCE!

There's a point in most people's training where getting the trick right in an empty room stops being the whole story. You start wondering what it would actually be like under show lights, with people watching you.

Open Rig Night is where you find out. You don't need a finished act, a costume or years behind you — just something you're curious enough to put in front of people. Talk to your instructor, or to Mark or Yara directly, and they'll help you work out what you're ready to bring and how to get it there.

Youth Circus performers open the night, then it moves through pro artists trying out new material, instructors, and students from the full-time courses and the weekly classes alike. It happens to land partway through this year's autumn 4-Week Intensive too, but the night's never been just for course students — that's always been the shape of the bill: everyone training here, not one group specifically.

Three weeks is enough time to ask your instructor the question, and still enough time to do something about the answer. If you'd rather watch than get up there yourself, that's the whole point of an audience.

TWO POLE OPTIONS AT THE EDGE

HELEN AND MICHELLE TEACH FLY AND STATIC POLE

Fly pole and Chinese pole share a word and some techniques but there's a world of difference between them. One moves with you, and sometimes against you, mischievously with a mind of its own. The other doesn't move at all and is demanding in all sorts of other ways.

Helen is teaching the first and Michelle the second at October Edge, giving you two different tracks with lots of scope for overlap, and therefore even more progress.

And the Edge festivals are all about exactly that – giving you the edge. October with three days straight and Easter with four, so that you embed and embody the techniques in your four chosen arts by drilling them on repeat.
