THE INVISIBLE ART OF STAGE FLIGHT

IT'S TECHNICAL AND INSTINCTIVE

I have been over at Tramway in Glasgow this week working with Birds of Paradise – an absolutely lovely company to be collaborating with.

I was brought in to work with the company on performer flying – to design and rig the flying system for the show, to train the performer, and to work with the cast and creative team to make sure the aerial effects feel like a natural part of the story.

During the shows I’m the one on the other end of the line, counterweighting the performer and responsible for safety for this part of the act.

As a rigger, we need to consider the right technical solution for the show as well as how it will impact the performer, the cast and the show itself. Both the team at Tramway and Birds of Paradise have been tremendous to work with to make it work and to ensure safety at all times.

If this is something you would like to learn, there’s an entire afternoon dedicated to performer flying in the upcoming Rigging for Circus Arts course.

Seeing a production come together is also a great reminder of what happens when you commit to an intensive block of training – like at the Easter Edge. You won’t regret it.

Mark Gibson

Aerial Edge Founder

It's better in person

There is some debate about whether you can master circus arts just by following video tutorials. The internet is a great library, but training is so much better in person.

Apart from the social aspect, a screen can’t see your form, feel your momentum, or shout a correction before a bad habit becomes part of your muscle memory.

Our Easter Edge coaches will act as human sensors, tracking your anatomy and the physics of the move in real time, to make the most of the four-day experience. They make sure your foundations are solid, giving you the safe exits and technical precision that a tutorial simply cannot provide.

The four-day breakthrough

This is where the immersion of the Easter Edge becomes your secret weapon across every discipline. Whether you are working on the internal GPS of handstand workshops or the explosive dynamics of the aerial sessions, four days in a row is a total game changer.

This is when you think less and do more. It's like learning a language by moving to the country instead of just using an app.

You don't have time for doubt to grow or muscle memory to go cold. You pick up on the morning exactly where you left off the night before. By Monday, moves that felt like a mountain on day one feel like second nature.

Join the feedback loop

Circus is a social scene. Come for the expert eyes, stay for the energy of the floor, and get your progress unstuck.

With Gabby back and the flying tracks doubled, these spots are going to move fast.

THE DEMAND FOR EXPERTISE

RIGGING AS A CREATIVE LANGUAGE

This week Mark has been working on a project that illustrates how rigging is so much more than spanners, ropes and carabiners. It’s at the heart of the creative process as Mark has been working with Birds of Paradise Theatre Company on their show, (We indulge in) a bit of roll play at Glasgow’s Tramway.

Mark was brought in to design and rig the performer flying system. There’s a lot more to that than load calculations and pulley systems. He’s spent the week collaborating and rehearsing with the creative team to get the visual effects exactly right.

During the performances, Mark is counterweighting the flyer, providing the physical timing and resistance that makes the movement look effortless on stage. It is a role where technical maths meets creative intuition.

Our specialised rigging course

This Tramway project is a great example of the real-world application of what we cover in our comprehensive four-day Rigging for Circus Arts course.

Spread over two weekends, this course is designed for those who want to move beyond the basics and understand the serious physics of the rig. It’s for the performers and instructors who want to be in demand for their technical expertise.

  • Part 1: Building the foundation of safety, loads, and essential mechanics.

  • Part 2: Designing and building complex rigging systems, rescue operations and getting into specialised areas, including a full afternoon dedicated to performer flying.

In that flying session, you will look at the types of systems Mark is using at Tramway – learning how to build them, how to counterweight effectively, and how to maintain the crucial line of communication between the ground and the air.

You can do the first weekend as a standalone course, or do both to make rigging your career. This will cover all that you need to work in many industries, but most industry rigging courses don't cover the specific knowledge you need to work in circus.

SUPER SATURDAY TRIPLE BILL

DYNAMICS, RIGGING & ACRO PLAY

Great training can be about mastering tricks – if that’s what you want, we've got you sorted. You can also add extra layers to your experience by understanding the environment you move in. For that, you want Super Saturday!

You have three sessions available which, when put together, give you a 360-degree view of what it means to be a circus artist.

Freya’s dynamics: working with gravity

If you have been straining to muscle your way through transitions, it will not be surprising if you are hitting a ceiling.

Freya’s dynamic hoop and rope workshops give you the physics of the beat. No, this isn’t the kind of science lesson you loved or dreaded in high school…this is your body teaching your brain. When you embody the movement, you get it, and it clicks in your brain.

By learning to work with gravity rather than fighting it, you unlock moves that previously felt frustrating. When you find that weightless moment, the hardest skills become accessible.

Mark’s rigging: the language of the rig

For those who want to look under the hood of the circus machine, Mark is teaching rigging to a level that’s suitable for instructors, performers, and people who train at home and want to be certain that the set-up is safe. And who doesn’t?

This is where you move from being a passenger to having a real understanding of the systems that make aerial work possible. Whether you are curious about load calculations or want to understand the mechanics of different set-ups, this course gives you a technical foundation that stays with you every time you look up at a rig.

Acro jam: the social lab

Progress can happen when the structure of a class is removed. The acro jam is your laboratory.

It’s where you take the technical cues from classes and see how they play out in a social, collaborative setting. It’s often in these informal exchanges that the biggest lightbulb moments happen.
