FROM SMALL JOBS COME AWESOME SIGHTS

WHY WE DON'T NEED A TANNOY ON SHOW NIGHT

In a theatre, a show starts being a show well before the audience arrives. Stage management call the half-hour on the backstage tannoy, then the quarter, then five minutes, and last of all beginners, which fetches whoever opens the show to the stage. Each call tells one group of people where they should be.

We have no tannoy. What we have in advance is a tech run-though for lighting, audio and rigging; chairs positioned safely out of range of dynamic aerialists; youth performers being wrangled into warm-ups by instructors who morph into riggers and performers; people vying for a corner of the bathroom mirror to apply make-up; and A LOT of high-energy excitement from pro and student performers, some of whom might be stepping on to that stage for the first time. So calling for beginners would be a mistake even if we had a tannoy. First-timers aren't always first in the running order.

A smooth-running show is mostly small jobs done in the right order before anyone sits down. Since we arrived in Govan three years ago, the number and type of small jobs has changed, as we have added more expertise and tech to the infrastructure, which has hugely improved the quality of the experience for everyone.

I'm grateful as always to Aerial Edge team and volunteers who take on these jobs, and make the show happen. We don't even need a tannoy, because people show up, pull together and collaborate seamlessly.

I'm proud to be part of it, and hope you will be there to be part of it too, cheering performers on. If not, you can follow this newsletter and Instagram to see the photos and videos that will emerge.

Mark Gibson
Aerial Edge Founder

FROM NEUTRAL TO RAZZLE DAZZLE

THE TRANSFORMATIVE EFFECT OF OPEN RIG NIGHT

American vaudeville often put its acrobats on first, while the audience was still finding seats, because the opener was an act that wouldn't be spoiled by the bustle of an audience settling in.

We wouldn't do that to our Open Rig Night performers, even if they were capable of handling it, because everyone deserves the audience's full attention.

First on stage will be the Youth Circus performers, and the audience will be seated and settled before the spotlight hits the first of them. If you arrive late, you wait behind the curtain until the next act.

By day, our space is neutral and calming: grey mats, grey walls, the steel of the truss. Then high-spec lighting and sound are deployed for the show, and the room becomes a performance space with more razzle dazzle per square inch of stage than a magpie's jewellery box.

The show has the same effect on people. They transform from your training buddy into a star in front of your eyes.

This show has three segments, with more than 30 performers across them. After Youth Circus comes the adult section, among them pros like Elke Bucher on fly pole, current students on the full-time courses, former students from those courses, and students from the recreational evening classes. The final third belongs to students and instructors of flying trapeze, our most popular class type.

It is a different kind of evening from our Foundation Course ensemble show, which is a directed piece, built around a small group of people, each of whom shines their own light before they come together for the parts that hold the show up.

Open Rig Night is chock full of different characters, bringing their own unique version of circus to you. Everyone at Aerial Edge is encouraged to consider performance, so you see all kinds of students who have found the confidence to step on stage and do their thing.

That means no two Open Rig Nights are the same. Almost all of the acts will be performed for one night only, and it never disappoints.

So how can you miss it?

IT'S TIME TO GET THE EDGE

BOOK OCTOBER EDGE TRACKS AT 1PM TODAY

At 12.58pm today, lots of people will be on a sofa with a laptop on their knees, a cup of tea going cold and fingers hovering over the keyboard. It's October Edge track booking day.

If you haven't already bought your three-day pass, that's top of your to-do list before lunch. You need that in place before you book your tracks.

Our Edge weekend festivals are a great way to move the needle on your capabilities. Training the same four arts every day for three days straight (or four at Easter) is like how a path forms across a lawn: crossing on the first day barely flattens the grass, and by the third there is a well-worn path that you could follow in the dark.

The path in this situation is your neural pathways. They get established, and your body knows its way through the moves that have been challenging you.

At 1pm today you book the tracks you've been eyeing up in the schedule above. That will be your schedule for every one of the three days. So make a strategy.

Don't start at 10am and work your way through the day if your most-wanted workshop is in the afternoon. Make a list of the arts you want most, in order, and ask yourself which one you'd be devastated to miss. Get ready to book that one first, no matter what time it is in the day.

It works like packing for a trip, where the thing you'd hate to forget goes in first and the spare socks go in whenever.

The deciding is the hard part, and it ends when the last art is booked. A Harvard study found something that might cheer you if you have decision fatigue or anxiety.

Photography students who were told their choice of print was final came to like it more than students who were allowed to swap. No one had expected that. Once the decision was made, they ended up liking what they'd chosen.

That's a nice thought to take into 1pm if you find the workshop you wanted is full. Just pick something else and move on, in the knowledge that you'll end up liking it.

On Friday 23rd the warm-up starts at 9.30am, and by then the choosing will be something that happened to somebody on a sofa a couple of weeks ago...you'll be raring to go.

ALERT AND AIRBORNE

DO AERIAL AND SLEEP SOUND

At 9.30pm on a weekday, most of Glasgow is negotiating with itself about one more episode. At Aerial Edge, classes are still going, and the people in them are rather more alert than the negotiators.

Finishing at 10pm gives some people pause. But the idea that evening exercise impairs sleep was debunked in a 2019 review in Sports Medicine which pooled 23 studies. In fact, the opposite was true.

The caveat was vigorous exercise ending within about an hour of bedtime. Whether a class counts as vigorous depends on the class and on you, and the research can't say. Sounds more like Zumba than aerial activities. By the time you get home and sort yourself out with a drink/cocoa/epsom salt bath, the land of nod is likely to be yours to inhabit.

So check out your options: Flying trapeze on Mondays and Wednesdays. Fly Fundamentals with Nea on Tuesdays, or mixed aerial with Kerri - that's loops, hammock and chains.

And Hamish returns from London this week, so he's taking over Wednesday fly classes.

By the time they finish, the street outside will have gone quiet, and you'll be one of the few people on it who spent the evening in the air, with considerably greater sense of satisfaction than anyone who negotiated an extra episode.

SORRY, NO CAMP THIS HALF-TERM

BUT YOUTH PERFORMERS STAR IN OPEN RIG NIGHT TONIGHT

The Youth Circus Camp planned for next week isn't going ahead. We know that's disappointing. Some children had been looking forward to it, and some parents had planned a week around it. We're sorry. If you booked, we've already been in touch.

Aerial Edge is a not-for-profit, so we keep prices as low as we can and we do on occasion run some things that don't pay for themselves, as an investment in our community.

But a camp needs instructors cleared for the whole week and a group to plan around. At some point, we have to decide if it's viable. On the booking system, a family which is still thinking about it looks exactly like a family which isn't.

It's a bit like hosting a supper where everyone replies "maybe". You still have to decide how much soup to make.
