EVERYTHING CHANGES ALL THE TIME

NOTHING IN CIRCUS OR SPORT STANDS STILL

There’s a lot happening in the city right now. Sport at world level, streets full of visitors, and a festival programme threaded through it all, including our Circus For All events and hundreds of others.

Last Saturday we stepped into the city with our first pop-up at St Enoch Centre. It’s been a reminder of something we see every day in the school – how quickly people move from “I could never do that” to actually doing it, laughing, balancing, trying again.

That shift never really gets old.

At the same time, there’s always something new happening in our space. Our ever-evolving schedule of classes and workshops provides something extra to brighten your days next week, and our Foundation Course students are preparing a fantastic show for you, Whispers of Old.

As the Commonwealth Games Festival continues across Glasgow, we’re proud to be part of something that isn’t just about watching excellence, but widening the circle of who gets to experience movement in the first place.

Mark Gibson

Aerial Edge Founder

CIRCUS FOR ALL IN THE CITY

OUR COMMONWEALTH GAMES FESTIVAL EVENTS

Last Saturday at St Enoch Centre, we launched the first of our Circus For All events as part of the Glasgow 2026 Commonwealth Games Festival – and it felt just like what we'd hoped it would be.

Circus landed in the middle of everyday life. Shoppers paused to try aerial silks, partner acrobatics, hula hooping, and plate spinning. People stepped out from surrounding shops on breaks, families wandered in, and across the afternoon we saw something simple and powerful – people of all ages trying circus, together, without hesitation or hierarchy.

It sits alongside the Commonwealth Games in an interesting way. While elite athletes from across the world are gathering to compete for gold, Circus For All is gathering people from across the city to step into movement for the sake of experience itself – to try, explore, and discover what’s possible.

The next Circus For All pop-ups are coming up at Govan Cross on Saturday 6th June, and Silverburn on Saturday 4th July. It's all leading towards our main showcase on 25th July, where we bring the programme together in performance. After the show, the performers and our instructors will host 30-minute taster classes across a much wider range of circus arts – including flying trapeze – giving the audience a chance to move from watching to doing in a single afternoon.

Julia & Robyn in the Festival film

When Glasgow stepped in last moment in to host the Commonwealth Games, it could have meant a focus purely on competition – stadiums, schedules, medals, results. But this is a city that loves a party, so there’s a festival running from now until after the Games, to make it something that’s lived, not just watched.

We worked with producers of the festival film, Your Invitation to Gather, to be part of this wider story – and you can see our instructors Julia and Robyn representing Aerial Edge in the film above.

The Commonwealth Games Festival is a city-wide programme of events from community groups, artists, and organisations that turns the whole period into something bigger than sport alone, it includes people, movement, and culture at every level.

GET INTO YOUR SWING

5 REASONS WHY YOU NEED SWING TECHNIQUE CLASS

Nea's new Tuesday Fly Fundamentals class focuses on the hidden mechanics that make flying trapeze far more satisfying. Here’s why you want to come to this class:

1. Bigger & better: Good technique creates height, power, and that delicious feeling of properly flying through the arc instead of just surviving the next beat.

2 Less effort: The magic is less about muscling the swing, and more about learning how to ride it.

3 More catches: Your swing is the engine behind catches. You gotta get high enough to float down into the catcher’s grip.

4 Stronger returns: Turnarounds and going back to the platform don’t have to be like an aerial argument between all your body parts and gravity. You can make it sharp and calm.

5 Cradle to brave: Learning the basics of basing builds trust, timing, and a deeper understanding of how flying trapeze really works.

SPY GAMES, STORIES & SUMMER SKIES

FROM IN-CLASS SHOWINGS TO SUMMER CAMPS

Our Youth Circus teens recently shared their in-class showing with family and friends – and what stood out wasn’t just what they did in the air, but how they chose to tell the story.

Rather than showing a tricks-led series of solo acts, they leaned into character and narrative. They had argumentative spies, grumpy country leaders, shifting alliances – all delivered with a mix of humour in rehearsal and real commitment once the lights came on.

The creative instinct in this group meant that the aerial and acro work sat inside the story rather than leading it, like sparks of action woven through a larger world they had created together. All they needed was a bit of guidance on structure.

That same spirit carries straight into our two Summer Camps during the school break. From Monday to Friday, 10am–4pm, every day includes flying trapeze (always the favourite), aerial arts, acrobatics, object manipulation, and a daily freedom session to explore, play, and create together.

It’s structured enough to build skills, but open enough for imagination to run.

You can book individual days, or book all five and get one day free.
