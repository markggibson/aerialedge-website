## WHY SMALL IS VALUABLE

## FROM GOVAN TO THE STAGE, TO WHAT'S NEXT

Circus keeps teaching us is that progress rarely looks dramatic while it's happening.

It happens in the small shifts. The second attempt that feels steadier, the moment something clicks, the times when something that once felt impossible quietly becomes normal.

We see it in every class, across every age group.

Someone who wouldn't leave the floor starting to climb. Someone who said "I can't do handstands" finding balance for a few seconds longer each week. Someone arriving unsure and leaving with a plan for what they want to try next time.

That's what keeps this worthwhile. Not perfection, but change.

So whether you're here for fitness, full-time training, youth classes or just trying something new after our Commonwealth Games Festival events, there's no need to arrive already able to do everything.

Just arrive willing to begin. The rest tends to take care of itself.

Mark Gibson

Aerial Edge Founder

## THE ART OF FALLING BEAUTIFULLY

## THERE ARE REASONS WHY SILKS IS ICONIC

When you think of circus, you think of silks. That's how long-term Aerial Edge student Cath sees it. A multi-disciplinary aerialist who has performed in shows including Jesus Christ Superstar at the King's Theatre, Cath explains here why silks remains one of her favourite arts...and why it might appeal to you.

You don't have to start high up
It's a complex and challenging discipline but even from your first class, there's lots you can practice close to the ground, so you can get off to a good start. You can work with knots, wraps and shapes to learn tricks, and separately build your climb skills, then combine the two so you're doing cool tricks up high.

It gets you strong fast
"You notice the difference really quickly," Cath says. "It improves core strength, builds grip, back and limb strength, without developing bulging muscles. Another surprise advantage is that it's low impact."

It's beautiful
The climbs, the twists, the way performers seem to float through the air. "It's mesmerising," says Cath. "And it's iconic, partly thanks to Pink being a silks artist."

It's clever
"It's keeping you mentally agile as well as physically," says Cath. "Those beautiful moves are wrapping the fabric in a way that the audience can't see you are creating your safe end point." While the audience sees elegance and drama, the artist is thinking ahead, setting up pathways and preparing for big moves while staying safe.

And then there are the drops…

It's like an illusion
"You get up into the air wrapping and balancing as you go, then suddenly drop fast, and people aren't quite sure how you've done it," says Cath. "It is a wickedly rewarding moment when you perform a drop and hear the audience gasp."

Which, perhaps, is the real appeal of silks. The more you learn, the more magical it becomes.

## IT'S JUST A ROPE. UNTIL IT ISN'T.

## THE DISCIPLINE THAT LIKES A PROBLEM

Not every aerial discipline attracts the same sort of person.

Some people love the spin and flow of hoop. Some are drawn to the drama of trapeze.

And then there are the rope people.

Long-time student Sammy is one of them. "It's physically demanding," he says. "I enjoy the problem solving, doing the trick without getting trapped!"

That might sound like an odd selling point, but rope has always had a reputation as the aerial discipline for people who enjoy a puzzle.

With only a single line of apparatus to work with, there are fewer places to hide than on silks and fewer shortcuts to a finished position. The reward is a discipline that develops incredible body awareness, coordination and strength.

"It uses different areas of the body to my other discipline, flying trapeze," says Sammy. "And there's always something new to learn or improve, so there's always a reason to come back."

Of course, not everyone falls in love with rope immediately.

"I don't like it!" admits Moira. "I can't climb."
Which is exactly why she's booked into Mixed Aerial this Sunday.

Perhaps that's another thing rope people have in common. They don't necessarily enjoy finding things easy. They enjoy figuring them out.

Come try it. Worst case, you've got a fun story to tell.

## NEW 6-MONTH COURSE STARTS AUGUST

## A PRO SHOW REEL THAT TAKES YOU FURTHER

We're introducing a new structure for our full-time training pathway.

The Six-Month Professional Development Programme (PDP) replaces our Professional Development Course, which lasted an academic year.

The PDP runs alongside the Four-Month Foundation Course (FC), with both starting on the same intake dates twice a year.

For some students, the FC is the right journey – a complete intensive block that includes technical training, a solo performance at Open Rig night around weeks 6–7, and finishes with a professionally directed ensemble show.

At present, show reels are filmed at the end.

However, the final weeks of FC are primarily focused on ensemble creation, which can limit the time available to develop work for film.

The PDP changes this.

A show reel is a circus artist's calling card. You can use it to get hired. You can use it to audition for a degree course in circus arts.

Instead of filming immediately after the ensemble, PDP students take an additional two-month block to return to their solo practice with fresh perspective. Using everything gained through ensemble work, they refine, rebuild and elevate their act.

This final stage focuses on act development, choreography, character, musicality, and performing for camera as well as live audiences, alongside technical exploration such as lighting, rigging and projection.

The result is a more intentional, more refined show reel.

Some students will complete Foundation and move on. Others will continue into the PDP. Both pathways are valid, they simply lead to different outcomes.

## WHY YOU'LL LOVE ELKE'S FLY POLE

## WHAT SHE DOES IN THE ROOM

Elke Bucher's fly pole workshops sell out. Not because of our marketing — because of what happens during the four hours you're with her.

Watch her teach and four things stand out.

She starts with safety, and builds.
Every workshop opens with safe entries and climbs. Then technique. Then the dizziness work — drills borrowed from ballet and aerials that teach your head how to find still points while your body keeps spinning. Only then do the combos arrive. The sequencing is methodical, never rushed, and you can feel her watching the room the whole way.

She adapts the combos to who's there.
Three levels and a Choreography & Flow session across the day. Inside each, the combos shift — variations, regressions, extra challenge options — so the work you do is the work you can do well, and the work you do next is the work that pulls you forward. Returning students level up. New movers find the version that fits.

She brings the dance to the apparatus.
Fly pole is a spinning apparatus; in Elke's hands it's a partner. Her signature style is strong ethereal tricks with a strange balance between soft and loud, angry music — and that aesthetic threads through her teaching. The choreography session at 3pm is where this opens out fully: floor-to-air rhythm, musicality, transitions, the apparatus as something you're dancing with rather than something you're conquering.

She has done the work.
Elke has placed in Miss Pole Dance UK semi-pro and multiple editions of Pole Sports Org. She has performed for Steel Panther in Germany and at the House of Serpents in London. She teaches at Pole Place Glasgow, Glasgow Uni and Strathclyde Uni. None of that is why students come back — they come back for what's in the room — but it's the reason her room knows what to do with whatever you bring into it.

Elke's approach is to meet people where they are, then make them slightly braver than they expected. That is why the workshops sell out.

## ACRO JAM

## SCOTLAND'S ACRO COMMUNITY MEETS MONTHLY

Acro Jam is the Saturday afternoon room where Scotland's acro community comes together to train, play, and put each other up in the air for three hours.

The mix is the point. Regulars working on their handstands next to beginners feeling out their first counter-balance. Pairs trying a new wash. Someone watching, asking, coming over to spot.

It's not a teaching session, this is where you practice and consolidate what you've been learning.

By 3:30 you've worked on shapes, met friends and people you didn't know, and seen what acro looks like a few rungs ahead of where you're standing.

## SUMMER CAMPS ARE THE ANSWER

Instead of watching adventures on a screen, how about kids being the ones who are adventures themselves?

At Youth Circus Summer Camo, kids aren't controlling a character on a screen. They are the character – climbing, swinging, balancing, flipping and creating their own story in real time.

We have two camps during the school hols - each a full week of circus exploration, where children step into aerial, acrobatics, juggling, games and creative movement, all led in a way that builds confidence, coordination and imagination without them even noticing.

Some will discover they love being upside down. Others will surprise themselves with what they can climb, balance or perform. Most will end the week having tried far more than they thought they could on day one.

The kind that start with "you'll never guess what I did today…"

And the daily flying trapeze classes will often be the main source of chat.

It's physical, it's creative, and it's full of moments they'll be talking about on the way home.
