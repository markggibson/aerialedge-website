## HIGHER PERSPECTIVES

TRUE GROWTH THROUGH COMMUNITY

Outside, the world is full of predictable routines and horizontal paths – but in here, we operate on a different axis. Whether you are climbing a rope for the first time or finally finding the timing for a doubles trapeze catch, you are doing something that defies the ordinary.

While we are all working on our own goals, we are never truly training alone. When someone pops a drop they have been working on, the cheer that goes up from others in the space is genuine. In circus, we all know the effort required to make something look effortless.

As we look toward Easter Edge, I encourage you to lean into this community. An intensive is more than just extra hours on the equipment, it intensifies that atmosphere of encouragement and collective progress.

When you train alongside others for four days straight, the support system becomes as vital as the rigging. It creates a space where you can be braver and push past your perceived limits, knowing you are surrounded by people who want to see you succeed.

Let’s keep reaching for that higher perspective together.

Mark Gibson

Aerial Edge Founder

## WEIGHTLESS BY DESIGN

FIND FLIGHT WITHOUT THE FIGHT

Dynamic rope and hoop with Freya return! Whether you’re new to dynamics or have the fundamentals down and want to expand your skills and understanding, these workshops will have something for you.

They're open-level sessions which will work through a range of dynamic pathways and satisfying fast-paced transitions.

Key themes will include:

-

Rope: Beat technique (e.g. front-back, bell, straddle), dynamic grip changes (e.g. hand slides and simple releases), turns, dynamic entries and transitions between familiar static poses, and different ways of finding momentum.

-

Hoop: Beat technique (e.g. front-back, bell, scissor), alternate grip beats (e.g. mixed grip, armpits), dynamic grip switches, drops, turns, transitions, and different ways of finding momentum as you exit familiar static poses to instantly supercharge your way into bigger swings

You’ll leave with snappy new combos as well as an increased understanding of mechanics to help you create your own custom dynamic combos going forward.

Expect a mental challenge as well as a physical one as we build your coordination and awareness in the air, unlocking weightless moments to help you move more efficiently and confidently.

If you train multiple aerial disciplines, this workshop will be beneficial beyond just your hoop practice, with beat form, twists, timing, and an understanding of ‘float’ points all being transferable and complementary to other disciplines – including flying trapeze.

Freya is a multidisciplinary circus artist specialising in hoop and rope and has performed and taught across the UK and abroad, working within both traditional and contemporary circus.

## KNOW YOUR LOAD

VITAL SAFETY FOR NON-RIGGERS

If you’re coaching, performing, or training at home, your equipment is your life-support system. But there is a dangerous gap between normal venue rigging and circus rigging.

Just because a venue has a rating for hanging lights and other equipment doesn't mean it can handle the dynamic, multi-directional forces of a 60kg aerialist, let alone the shock load of a decent drop.

Mark’s rigging workshop for instructors, performers and people who want to rig apparatus at home takes the mystery out of hardware and puts the safety in.

He isn’t training you to be a rigger, he’s training you to be a competent person who knows how to keep yourself and people in your classes safe, and that includes when to say "no" to a less than perfect set-up.

What we’ll cover:

-

The physics of flight: Understanding static vs dynamic loads – why a 60kg aerialist can easily exert multiple times their body weight of force during a move which can break rigging equipment.

-

The venue audit: Whether you are a venue owner, manager or a visiting performer, you need to know the right questions to ask, how to spot equipment that needs to be retired, the difference between industrial and recreational gear, and what to look for in a home rig.

-

The teacher’s eye: How to perform a safety sweep of equipment and identify wear and tear red flags before they become risks.

-

Professional performance: How to talk to venue technicians and ensure the rigging points provided meet the safety standards for your specific act.

-

Home training truths: The dos and don'ts of residential rigging (hint: it needs more than rawlplugs in a standard domestic ceiling). You’ll find out why bolting into a joist and suchlike is rarely enough and what structural strengthening actually looks like for a safe home set-up.

-

Inspections & silent killers: How to spot defects in metal and worrying wear and tear in textiles that a quick look can miss.

Rigging isn't just a behind-the-scenes skill. It is the foundation of your practice. This is about becoming a smarter, safer aerialist. It’s about knowing exactly what is happening above your head so you can focus on what you’re doing with your body.

If you are responsible for a class of students or your own safety, you cannot afford to stay in the dark. It’s time to get technically sound.

## Build connections, not just shapes

If classes are where you learn the grammar of acrobatics, the monthly Acro Jam is where you write the poetry.

There is a kind of social neuroplasticity in a jam. The constant variation builds a robust acrobatic practice. According to movement researchers, this kind of unstructured play is great for developing high-level problem-solving skills and spatial awareness – the kind that allows you to save a wobbly handstand or adjust a catch in real-time.

When the hierarchy of teacher and student disappears, it’s a chance to test the skills you’ve been drilling in class in a casual, creative environment.

There's no need to come with a partner, you’ll find flyers looking for bases, bases looking for flyers, and everyone looking for a spotter.

Come for the training, stay for the community, and leave with a smile of satisfaction.

## THE CURATED CIRCUS INTENSIVE

RADICAL PROGRESS IN RARE AND CLASSIC ARTS

By curating a mix of circus arts you can’t find on our weekly schedule alongside those you can, the Easter Edge is designed for breakthrough moments. This is where the Specialised meet the Classics.

It’s rare to find doubles trapeze on a regular circus school schedule – the logistics of timing, space, and partnership usually make it a special-occasion discipline. And the Easter Edge is just such an occasion.

The four-day festival can be a frenzy of fun, or a strategic training intervention. It’s your choice how you want to play it.

This year, specialised arts pathways include Kerri’s tippy hoop and hammock as well as Mark and Yara’s double trapeze. There may be others – we'll announce what's on the schedule in the weeks to come.

"I like being thrown around in the air by others," Yara says. "Doubles disciplines are such a good way to connect with people. We’ll be working with each student depending on their level, moving from foundational sequences right through to dynamic releases."

This is a unique window to make significant headway in an art form that relies on trust and timing.

The compounding effect

While the specialised arts offer a new perspective, the core of Easter Edge is about what happens when you train the classic arts on the regular weekly schedule with four days of unbroken focus.

In a standard once-a-week class, some of your time is spent "re-remembering" what your body learned seven days prior. At the intensive, that lag time vanishes.

-

The magic of immersion: Because you’re back on the same apparatus every 24 hours, your muscle memory compounds rather than fades. You’ll find that the sticky points in your technique finally resolve into a fluid architecture. Bonus: work in one art will pay off in another.

-

Refined technical logic: Frequent repetition helps your brain map out the geometry of complex wraps and transitions. Your body systems stay switched on, allowing you to refine the structural logic of a move until it becomes second nature.

-

Instinctual movement: Moving from conscious thought to muscle memory and physical instinct happens significantly faster in a concentrated environment.

Why train doubles?

Training doubles isn't just solo trapeze with a friend. It’s a different mechanical and psychological game.

Beyond the spectacle, doubles work offers a neuromuscular upgrade that solo training can’t replicate. When you add a second body to the bar, you enter a world of kinaesthetic resonance – the phenomenon where you feel the physical sense of someone else's movement within your own body.

-

Shared momentum: In solo trapeze, you only manage your own weight. In doubles, you must learn to read your partner’s centre of gravity as if it were your own. This builds an elite level of spatial awareness and proprioception.

-

The trust loop: Research into partner acrobatics shows that high-stakes collaboration reduces cortisol and builds non-verbal literacy – meaning you learn to communicate through touch, tension, and timing. These skills make you a more sensitive, reactive aerialist when you return to your solo practice.

-

Physics of the catch: You’ll move from static poses into the mechanics of the beat, discovering how to generate 10 times the force of your body weight through synchronised timing rather than brute strength.

Whether you’re a seasoned aerialist or have never shared a bar, Mark and Yara will help you bridge the gap between hanging and harmony.

## FROM GRADES TO GREATNESS

WHERE HALF TERM HITS NEW HEIGHTS

Half-term circus camp is a welcome break from the classroom, yet it’s also a concentrated environment for building confidence. Like our four-day Easter Edge festival for adults, it makes for leaps and bounds in all sorts of ways.

We move away from the rigid structure of academic grades and competitive sports, into a space where the focus is on personal progression and creative problem-solving. It helps develop a resilient mindset that young people carry back into their everyday lives.

Oh, did we say it's all about fun? The benefits all happen by accident while the students are enjoying themselves.

-

Cognitive flexibility: Circus requires a unique blend of physical coordination and mental strategy. Research into movement-based learning suggests that activities requiring cross-lateral movement – like juggling or coordinated aerial sequences – strengthen the neural pathways between the brain's hemispheres, improving focus and executive function.

-

The resilience loop: In the circus, we celebrate the drop. Learning to fail safely and try again is the core of the practice. This builds a growth mindset, teaching students that competence is something earned through persistence rather than an innate talent.

-

Social architecture: Camp life is built on mutual support. Whether it is spotting a peer in acrobatics or encouraging someone on the trapeze, students develop a high level of social literacy and collaborative trust.

Your child won't just come home with a circus skills, they will have a different sense of what they are capable of achieving. It is three days of high-energy, high-reward training that turns a week off into a massive step forward.
