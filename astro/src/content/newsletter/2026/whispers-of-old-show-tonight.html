HANG OUT AT DIFFERENT BARS

RUSSIAN BAR, TEETERBOARD, AND MORE

If Glasgow is going to insist on treating summer as a loose suggestion rather than a season, we're taking matters into our own hands.

We're turning Aerial Edge into a three-day summer circus playground in July. That'll put some sizzle into our lives even if it's not barbecue weather.

Unlike our Easter and October Edge festivals, this one isn't about committing to a training track and watching your skills evolve over a weekend. You can absolutely do that if you want to. But you can also drop in, try something you've never touched before, spend a few hours getting launched into the air and celebrate a success.

Leading the charge are two disciplines we rarely get to offer – Russian bar and teeterboard. Flying trapeze is added to Saturday and we're giving you cradle on one of the Friday and Sunday scheduled flying trapeze classes, and we can throw in an acro jam if people are keen.

 

Russian bar

This involves two bases, a flexible pole and a flyer on said pole discovering that while gravity can momentarily be more of a guideline than a rule, it will get you in the end (safety lines will mitigate the potential for disaster). The goal is to work with the two bases to start and land on your feet on the bar – what you do in between is where the excitement lies.

 

Teeterboard

This is the circus version of a seesaw, except you stand not sit on it, and somebody aims to end up several feet higher than the end they were standing on.

 

Cradle

There are so many flyers who are throwing great big tricks and a few people who want to base in cradle. Put them together and you get tricks, swings on the base and returns to platform. And by heck, there's surely no one who doesn't love being able to do that. So let's make it happen!

It's our festival, in our home, built around the simple idea that summer should contain at least one story that begin with "you'll never guess what I did this weekend". And yes, it is also aligned with our loftier goal of developing people through circus.

TAKE IT EASY WITH TRAPEZE

THE BAR IS FRIENDLIER THAN IT LOOKS

Ever looked at a static trapeze and quietly decided it wasn't for you? A lot of people do...right up until they get on one.

This is an aerial art you can ease into, it doesn't demand much of you at the start. You can set it low, and you can sit on it, so you'll learn from a position that already feel stable.

There's no climbing a rope until you've earned the right to hang upside down, no need to launch yourself into the air, and you can let the bar do the lifting while you figure out what the heck your body is up to.

Some classes might be about building sequences, other weeks you might try doubles moves, which come with their own challenges. Laura, our regular student pictured above, says: "Doubles is a really fun session, full of belly laughs as you're trying to fit two adults on a small bar."

It's not especially tidy at first, but it doesn't have to be. Laura says: "There are lots of bloopers along the way and movements wondering how you have managed to tie yourself in a knot.

"At first you feel like a little elephant falling over your own feet, but after a few weeks confidence really can be built and moves become more expressive."

You gradually realise you're not just copying shapes and sequences, you're making choices. Laura says: "It's up to you and your imagination where these take you."

The pace is yours to choose. You're never expected to arrive knowing anything other than how to show up.

Laura adds: "It's a friendly class where you will be welcomed and encouraged by other students and instructors, so there is no need to worry about feeling out of place."

So, is trapeze for you? You'll never know until you try…

SET UP A SUMMER TO REMEMBER

THE FULL-ON WAY TO SPEND THE HOLIDAYS

While everyone is planning summer flights, queues, and scenic strolls around somewhere overcrowded, how about an adventure holiday that's way more satisfying?

Our Summer Intensive starts next Monday, 29th June, and you can hop on board for one week, two, three or the full four weeks. How much annual leave you got? Because this is a staycation like no other.

This is a refreshing getaway from most jobs – you'll train aerial and acro Monday to Friday from 10am til 5pm.

Crucially, you're not missing out if you can't do the full four weeks. This isn't an all-or-nothing situation, not even close.

A week or two is a blast, and it's enough to change how your body moves. That daily training rhythm embeds movement into your muscle memory and things start sticking in places they weren't sticking before.

A word of warning: we can't be held responsible if you don't want to leave.

Some people arrive with sensible intentions, and end up with new ones. We're not naming names, but we do leave space for it.

Fortunately, the four-month Foundation Course starts on Monday 10th August and so does the new six-month Professional Development Course, so there's something solid to return to.

A summer of training is a summer you remember.

MONDAY 10 AUGUST IS THE LAUNCHPAD

THREE COURSES, ONE START DATE

If you've been reading about the Summer Intensive as a kind of staycation with consequences, this is where that story continues.

On Monday 10th August, three courses share the same launchpad. The three groups of people step into the first month together, before their training stretches them in new directions.

If you're on the Four-Week Intensive, your training rhythm lasts long enough for it to stop feeling like a visit and start feeling like home. Like everyone else on the launchpad, you'll learn a broad range of aerial and acrobatic arts.

If you're staying for the four-month Foundation Course (FC) or the Six-Month Professional Development Programme (PDP), that pick & mix first month is about you sussing out what you like and why. Because when the four weeks are over, you'll be creating an act with your favourite art for Open Rig Night four weeks later, on Saturday 17th October.

After you've ridden the crest of that wave, you'll all work together with a professional director to weave either the same act into an ensemble show, or maybe you'll shift to a different discipline for that, or form a duo.

This moves beyond doing tricks in the same space – it's coordinating timing, intention, risk, and storytelling across multiple bodies at once.

And then when the curtain closes, you've said your farewells to your FC pals and you're on the PDC, the next two months are all about you.

The end goal is a pro show reel filmed by our amazing videographer Gabriel Stella, and there's A LOT of course content about how to bring your best to that. As well as refining skills, you'll learn to make tech like lights and projection to work for you, you'll dig into yourself for characterisation, you'll go way beyond an act and deep into art.

So the question isn't which course you should do. It's how far you want to go.

WE NOTICED SOMETHING. SETTLE IT FOR US.

Here's a thing we've clocked about our crowd. You'll grind a single trick for a full hour. You'll chase strength, the cleaner climb, that flag or that drop that's been fighting you for weeks. And then someone says the word "choreography" and the room goes very quiet.

So we're asking, plainly: would you come to a Chinese fly pole choreography workshop? A session about stringing your moves together — phrasing, musicality, making it flow instead of stacking tricks like a to-do list.

ACRO JAM JULY

DATE SHIFT TO MID-MONTH

Once a month we clear the floor, open the door, and run an open acro jam — three hours of solo, partner and group acrobatics with whoever turns up. July's round is on Saturday the 11th.

Here's how a jam works. There's no set ideas, no level you need to have reached, and no teaching. Bases find flyers, flyers find bases, and everyone practises what they want. You might spend the afternoon nailing a single wash, or you might end up in a three-high.

Bring a friend or come solo and make some. Acro is a team activity whether you planned it or not.

HAPPY CAMPER, OR SUMMER INTENSIVE?

TWO WAYS TO SPEND THE SUMMER

Some summer activities are for turning up and seeing what happens. Others are for seeing how far you can take it.

For young people, we run two very different kinds of experience, depending on age and appetite.

The Summer Camp is the bright, buzzy, slightly brilliant chaos of a week well spent. It's five days, 10am–4pm for ages 7–18, moving through flying trapeze, aerial, acro, games, and whatever else the coaches decide to unleash.

It builds through the week and ends with a Friday sharing for families – a snapshot of everything tried, learned, and occasionally improvised into success.

There are two week-long camps during the school hols, suitable for circus first-timers and returning campers.

Then there's the Summer Intensive – the "let's get somewhere with this" option.

It's a four-week course from next Monday 29th June til Friday 24th July, but you can do one-week blocks during that period.

It runs 10am til 5pm Monday to Friday for ages 16+ who want a different kind of summer rhythm. It's the same course our adult students take: daily training, repetition that starts to change how movement feels, and enough time on apparatus for real progression to kick in.

So it's simple: A brilliant week of flying, flipping and fun… or a focused stretch where things start to stick.

Both are good. Just different kinds of summer specials.
