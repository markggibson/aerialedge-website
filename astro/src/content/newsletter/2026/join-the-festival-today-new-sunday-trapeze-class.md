## TOUCHING THE FLOOR ISN'T CIRCUS SPLITS

## YOU CAN BE FLEXY AS YOU AGE...BUT IT TAKES MORE THAN STRETCHING

Getting your legs flat on the floor and holding a split there are two different skills. Only one of them survives being asked to do something in the air.

A couple of new blogs break down why. Range of motion gets you down, and might work if you're a dancer passing through that position.

But it's strength at the end of that range that lets you stay on the ground and hold it, and that's absolutely necessary to use it for anything once you're off the ground, or you risk injury from over-extending.

One article covers what actually moves the needle when you're attempting splits as an adult, the other is about gaining flexibility in your 50s, 60s and 70s. In both cases, it's more achievable than most people think.

If splits have been sitting on your someday list, this is worth five minutes before your next stretch session.

## ONE POINT, EVERY FORCE ON IT

## FORCES ON EVERY PART OF A SINGLE-POINT RIG, EXPLAINED

Most riggers can rig a single point. Fewer could tell you exactly where the load goes once it's up.

The newest article on Circus Rigging website takes on single-point aerial rigging – the forces acting on each part of the system, and how a 1:1 pulley setup behaves differently under load to a 3:1.

It's a 20-minute read, and it earns every minute. This is the kind of detail that separates a rig working from knowing why it works, and what happens to each component if something in the chain is wrong.

If rigging is any part of your circus life, it's worth the read before your next session.

## PERMISSION TO PROCRASTINATE

There's the washing you've been avoiding for so long that you have no fresh knickers, the PowerPoint that's sat three slides from finished since Tuesday, or the empty fridge that says you haven't got round to the supermarket.

Productivity-obsessed grind culture calls procrastination a personal failing you should fix with an app. But circus is different. So let's make it easy for you to make one decision – possibly the one that could change your life, and you don't even get time to properly consider it because your to-do list is two metres long.

Just do four weeks. That's the decision.

The Four-Week Circus Intensive Course is a complete package and a month of full-time training might be enough to make you feel it's got you somewhere, then you can move on, or move back to life the way it was before.

Then again, you might find it easy to decide that you want more – no problem. Your four weeks will have started on the same day as the Four-Month Foundation Course, and you'll all have been training a broad range of circus arts. So if you want to carry on training with your new friends, you can.

## NOBODY WANTS PICKED UP ON TIME

## LET THEM SHINE NOW, REWARDS WILL COME LATER

Nobody wants to be picked up on time from Youth Circus Summer Camps. It doesn't matter that a full day of upside-down adventures has left them barely able to lift their own arms by 4pm – they still think they've got enough left in them for one more go.

The proof is in the last half hour of the week, when family and friends come in for an informal showing. Everyone gets a turn to show off tricks and skills that they've learned, and they give it all they've got. The pride on display – on stage and in the audience – would warm the cockles of the coldest heart.

Kids elbowing their way to the front, wanting the people who matter to watch, is  well worth encouraging. It's the same impulse that gets someone up in front of an audience years later – whether it's as a circus artist, a singer, a powerpoint presenter or a lawyer in court.
