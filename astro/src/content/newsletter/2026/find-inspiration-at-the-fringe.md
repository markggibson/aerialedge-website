## GO GET LOST IN THE FRINGE

## THE DOORSTEP'S RIGHT THERE, USE IT

Every August, Edinburgh fills up with strangers trying to hand you a flyer, sometimes mid-cartwheel or somesuch larking around, always convinced their show is the one you shouldn't miss. You have to admire that passion, that belief in what we do is what brings ideas to reality and enhances the lives of others who experience them.

I'm heading through this month partly to feel inspired by new circus works, partly to catch up with old friends who are performing there this year.

It changes how you see your own training, and it's a reminder of what all the practice can actually lead to. Open Rig Night, anyone?

Choosing what to watch is the challenge, not squeezing into packed trains. If the flyers and the five-star quotes leave you stuck, Vee's picks below do the sorting for you. She's sat through enough of it to know which claims hold up.

Of course you can skip the shortlist and follow whichever poster made you stop walking. Half the fun of the Fringe can be the excitement on the streets and not being entirely sure what you've just bought a ticket for.

Mark Gibson
Aerial Edge Founder

## ROPE PLAYS HARD TO GET

## WHY ONE STUDENT DESPISED IT, UNTIL THEY COULDN'T STOP

"I hated rope at first," Foundation Course former student Max said. "It was awful, I despised it – it was the worst thing ever in my life. I was thinking, what are you doing to me?" Yet it also held a compulsion...so Max kept training.

Their final performance on the course ended up being rope, not the straps they'd arrived intending to do. And the next step on Max's circus development was spending a year at Creat circus school in Valencia, studying nothing else.

Freya Pellie, who's performed and taught around the UK (Aerial Edge's own Easter Edge included), remembers the same apparatus winning them over rather more easily: "There are so many things that can be done with it, so many different ways to move on it, so many different shapes." Where Max needed converting, Freya says rope just made sense.

Nea, who teaches rope at Aerial Edge every week, isn't fully sold on the usual comparison to silks. "There are some similarities," she says, "but ultimately rope is a whole thing of its own."

Read the full account on the blog. Grip battles, a Valencia detour and the rest of it – then come find out what "a whole thing of its own" means for yourself, in Nea's class.

## THE STAGE DOESN'T DEMAND PERFECT

## OPEN RIG NIGHT WANTS YOU ON IT, NOT JUST WATCHING

Somewhere between "I could never" and actually doing it, most people find out they were wrong about themselves. In the best way! And Open Rig Night is often where that discovery happens.

The show opens at 6.30pm with the Youth Circus students, all nerve yet little hesitation, then rolls straight into recreational students, full-time trainees and pros.

Nobody's slot matters more than anyone else's, everyone is sharing the same room and the same audience. A trick that landed that afternoon or one you've been polishing for a year get the same welcome from a supportive crowd who are there because they are willing you to succeed.

If there's something you've been working on at the back of a class, this is a reason to bring it into the open. You don't need your act to be finished. You just need it started, and October gives you enough runway to get there.

Prefer the seat over the spotlight? It'll still be a great night. But if any part of you has been circling a piece, an act, even one single trick you'd like someone to see: put your name down. The room will be on your side, with all of the Aerial Edge team leading the cheer.

## WHY ENGINEERS CALL IT IGNORANCE

## THE DELIBERATE GAP BETWEEN A SHACKLE'S STAMP AND ITS STRENGTH

Pick up a forged bow shackle and the bow reads WLL 1 t, stamped into the metal. A tonne, then. Except the shackle is built to take a long way more than that, and the gap between the stamp and the breaking point is there on purpose.

This week's article on circusrigging.info is about that gap. Engineers call it the factor of ignorance, because it exists to cover what nobody can see from the outside – variation in the metal, a hidden flaw in the forging, wear, the load that arrives harder than planned. You keep a margin for the things you can't inspect.

The history alone earns the six-minute read. There are Victorian engineers who trusted no casting they couldn't test, anchor-proving laws from the 1800s that still shape how lifting gear gets tested today, and the reason your oldest kit says SWL while everything newer says WLL.

Even the word shackle has a past, and it starts on a prisoner's wrist.

## WHAT'S WORTH YOUR TIME AT THE FRINGE THIS WEEK

Vee's back with four more from the Fringe. One of them's already halfway out the door, so move fast if you want to see it.

Top Acrobatics

Exit | Circumstances

I caught this show at a festival in Europe a few years ago, and I'm delighted it's coming to Edinburgh. A wonderful blend of dance and thrilling, top-quality acrobatics on a complex structure, Exit is well worth the early start time.

10.30am at Zoo Southside, running 18th until 30th August, 1hr, age 8+. Book [here](https://api.aerialedge.co.uk/email-tracking/71c583914f7?contactId={{contact.id}}&utm_source=email&utm_medium=email marketing)

Last Chance to Catch…

SAND | Kook Ensemble

Devon-based Kook Ensemble bring their beautiful show SAND to Summerhall. A touching and tender ensemble acro and juggling show about living with dementia, SAND uses the unstable nature of the eroding coastline to replicate the delicacy of memory.

1.30pm at Summerhall, running until Monday 17th August, 1hr, age 8+. Book [here](https://api.aerialedge.co.uk/email-tracking/afd5823ed83?contactId={{contact.id}}&utm_source=email&utm_medium=email marketing)

Adults Only!

The Kaye Hole | Hosted by Reuben Kaye

This infamous late-night cabaret features some of the finest and most raucous cabaret, comedy and circus you will see at the Fringe. Late, loud, lively and unmissable, this show is fabulous but definitely not for the faint-hearted.

11.30pm at Underbelly McEwan Hall, Fridays and Saturdays only, 1hr 30min, age 18+. Book [here](https://api.aerialedge.co.uk/email-tracking/b801cfc381b?contactId={{contact.id}}&utm_source=email&utm_medium=email marketing)

The Wing Scuffle Spectacular | The Revel Puck Circus

Returning to Edinburgh, The Revel Puck Circus bring all the classic joy of circus with a modern twist. Founded by graduates of the National Centre for Circus Arts in London, the company brings aerial, acro, teeterboard and more. Light-hearted and fun for all the family in the iconic Circus Hub on the Meadows, Wing Scuffle Spectacular is a great way to experience circus at the Fringe with the wee ones.
