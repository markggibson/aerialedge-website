PUT SOME SPRING IN YOUR SUMMER

THE PARTY APPARATUS IS COMING OUT TO PLAY

We're fed up cleaning cobwebs on the teeterboard. What it needs is an enthusiastic pounding to blow off the dust, so we've invented the Summer Festival purely to bring it back into commission. Seriously, that's how housework-averse we are.

It's been sitting there at the back of the room looking lonely for so long that it was pitiful, it broke our hearts. So we've reunited it with its old friend, the Russian bar. There were coughs and wheezing from the dust that came off that when we yanked it out of its slumbers.

Now, for those of you who aren't familiar with this pair, they are the good-time guys and gals of circus. They've got more party tricks in them than the Tartan Army at the World Cup.

Offering a potent cocktail of several spirited types of Newtonian physics with a shot of pure joy, whatever you give to them, they'll give you back, and more.

Put some downward force into the bar or the board, and they will reward you with the same force going upward. But because you're interacting with other people as well as the apparatus…well...whooooooosh!!! Wanna see how science helps you? See this blog!

And that's why we use safety belts and lines for the flyers, so you can let loose your reckless self without worrying about how gravity might get the better of you.

The Summer Festival is big on flying, with two flying trapeze sessions every day from Friday through Sunday as well as the teeterboard and Russian bar.

And there's a bonus Acro Jam happening all day Saturday. Just because.

It's free if you're taking any of the Summer Festival workshops, and if not it's the usual £12 for each of the two three-hour sessions.

You don't have to come for the whole weekend, all the workshops at the Summer Festival are bookable using any aerial multi-class pass. Want to do it all? There's a special All Access Festival Pass which means you can do 10 workshops at just £15 each.

MAGICAL MYTHICAL MOMENTS

WHISPERS OF OLD, IN PICTURES

You may have passed some of these people on the rig over the last four months, looking much the same as you and everyone else. Then last Saturday, they were mythic creatures.

Gabe's photos can't give you the room going quiet when the lights went up or the gasps when the big moves caught the audience unawares, but they'll give you a fair sense of what you missed – which is worth bearing in mind next time we stage a show.

Foundation Course shows are more than just demonstrations of skills acquired. They are high-quality contemporary circus performance. It's incredible what these students achieve in such a short time, with the guidance of Director Michelle Ross.

Four months ago, they were finding their feet on the apparatus as they embarked on their course, but you'd never have known that from your seat in the audience.

The thing worth understanding is that the show is never something bolted on at the end, after the training is done. It's the other way around – the show is what the whole course is built toward, and that changes how you train.

You learn to hold a moment differently when you know you have to move with other people, not just alongside them, and it's big achievement to carry something from an idea all the way to a stage.

An ensemble isn't a template for solos, it's a group of people who've learned to perform as one, and that's the specific thing this course exists to build.

So what you're looking at in these photos isn't the end of a training period, it's the reason for it. If you want to know more about how and why that's the case, see this blog.

The next Foundation Course starts Monday 10 August. If the photos make you wonder whether it's for you, follow that thought. Email us on info@contact.aerialedge.co.uk if you have any questions at all.

And remember, if you can train for six months, you can do the Professional Development Programme. You'd start with the Foundation Course then carry on for two months, focused back on your solo act, with a view to creating a banging showreel, shot by our amazing Gabriel Stella.

ELKE'S ROCKIN' HER CREATIVITY

FROM STAGE WITH STEEL PANTHER TO YOUR WORKSHOPS

Fly pole instructor Elke is on stage with Steel Panther in front of a crowd of thousands during their UK tour. And in a couple of weeks' time, she's bringing that same energy to four workshops that she's teaching here at Aerial Edge.

Elke has also performed circus shows in Guatemala and Mexico this year, taught a flying pole and lyra retreat in Mexico, and is appearing at Kelburn Garden Party and Flow in the Forest festival with her show, The Living Ecosystem.

The thread running through all of it, in her own words: "Getting out of my head and into creation."

Sounds good, and you can do that too, with her help.

She says: "The choreo workshop is aimed at all aerialists who like to feel and to spin in a way that's woven together by music to longer transitions. Taking it from the floor and then into the air, we'll learn how to incorporate the fly pole through pendulum swings, floorwork such as lunges and gentle backbends, then study rhythm and emotion for simple spins and mounts."

If you've ever finished a move and not known what comes next, this fills the gap. It's the creative side of the apparatus, the part that turns a list of tricks into a flowing piece.

There are still spaces in three of the four workshops Elke is teaching, and if you're into any of them, the choreo workshop makes a natural second half.

If you've never touched a pole in your life, don't hang back.

Elke says: "My beginner workshop strips it back to basics and looks at safely entering and spinning. We take breaks in between entries, tricks and combos and you can then merge your new skills together and take it further with some more height and progressions if you'd like.

"Flying pole can also build on transferable skills from pole dances as well as other aerial apparatus, including verticals for climbs and inverts.

"If you've never done any pole at all, you'd still have a ball on fly pole!"

THEY'LL LOVE THE FAMILY HOLIDAY

BUT THEN WHAT? HERE'S WHAT...

The family holiday is the big one — the trip everyone remembers. But it's a week or two, and then there's a long stretch of summer left, and a young person with energy to burn and not much to point it at.

That's the gap our youth circus fills. A week of Summer Camp is five days of aerial, acro, and whatever else the coaches line up — the kind of week a young person talks about long after it's done.

It's not about keeping them busy. It's about giving them a thing they're proud of, skills they didn't have on Monday and do by Friday, and a room full of others doing the same.

So when the holiday's over and the "I'm bored" starts, this is the answer. Keep the summer going. Keep them making memories.
