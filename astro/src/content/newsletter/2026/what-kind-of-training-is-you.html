THE AUDIENCE GETS A TURN

THANKS FOR SATURDAY, NOW HAVE YOUR SAY

Thank you to everyone who made Open Rig Night a huge success by any measure...the performers under the lights, the backstage team scurrying around behind the lights, and the audience sitting in the dark looking into the lights. It was our biggest production yet.

The number of people who wanted a turn on that stage, and the number of people who turned up to see them, is a good test of how Aerial Edge is serving its community.

Performing for a live audience isn't compulsory but we encourage everyone to consider it because it's a heck of a sense of achievement when you're done it. And now more people than ever have felt it.

When 38 people sign up, it's got to be a good thing. For them, and for the audience – where else can you get that much value? We're particularly proud that every sector of our community was represented: Youth Circus, adult recreational class students, current full-time course students, course graduates, instructors and pro performers.

No wonder 100 people bought tickets to see it.

Side note: why do you not believe us when we say Open Rig Nights sell out?! Still we get frantic last-ditch requests for tickets. The old saying "Book early to avoid disappointment" applies.

So if you were there, I'd like to ask one small favour, if you have a minute. A Google review of the show – or your favourite class – helps people find us and get an idea of what goes on here. It can be just a quick click to give a star rating, or leave a comment if you feel that could be your good deed for the day.

Mark Gibson
Aerial Edge Founder

WHICH EDGE COULD BE YOU?

FOUR ARTS, CURATED FOR YOUR TEMPERAMENT

On Monday morning, a colleague will probably ask if you had a good weekend. The usual answers are the conversational equivalent of a beige carpet.

October Edge starts next Friday, so the following Monday you'll have a full technicolour conversation lined up, and photographic evidence on your phone to back it up. You'll be hanging about the water cooler just daring people to ask what you got up to.

To help you choose the four workshops that would suit you best, here are some curated pathways based on character types.

1. The body-challenge seeker
10am: Aerial rope (Nea)
11.45am: Straps and loops (Robyn)
2pm: Fly pole (Helen)
3.45pm: Trampoline (Sam)
Why it suits you: Three arts that ask a lot of your body, then a trampoline at the end of the day for the part of you that still wants to play.

2. The sociable one
10am: Acrobatics (Sam)
11.45am: Flying trapeze (Hamish and Kirsty)
2pm: Fly pole (Helen)
3.45pm: Doubles trapeze (Mark)
Why it suits you: Waiting your turn on flying trapeze is where conversations start, acrobatics is a group activity, and doubles trapeze has the word "doubles" right in the name, so you're not alone, plus partners tend to swop within the class.

3. The one who wants a break from their own brain
10am: Tippy hoop (Kerri)
11.45am: Aerial hammock (Kerri)
2pm: Handstands (Sam)
3.45pm: Trampoline (Sam)
Why it suits you: Nobody has ever answered email from a trampoline and you can't hold your phone when your body weight is on your hands. Tippy hoop isn't on the regular weekly schedule so that's full of new skills to keep your mind busy, and don't be fooled into thinking hammock bears any relation to lounging around between two coconut trees – it's surprisingly demanding!

4. The cautious first-timer
10am: Acrobatics (Sam)
11.45am: Aerial hammock (Kerri)
2pm: Handstands (Sam)
3.45pm: Partner and group acro (Robyn)
Why it suits you: Three of the four are on or near the floor, the fourth is a hammock (a shape nobody finds alarming), and Sam is there for two of them so there's a familiar face by Friday afternoon.

5. The creative, artistic one
10am: Aerial rope (Nea)
11.45am: Straps and loops (Robyn)
2pm: Silks (Kirsty)
3.45pm: Doubles trapeze (Mark)
Why it suits you: Rope, straps and silks are very different ways to make shapes in the vertical axis rather than horizontal, and doubles trapeze adds a second person to make shapes with.

6. The perfectionist
10am: Aerial rope (Nea)
11.45am: Straps and loops (Robyn)
2pm: Handstands (Sam)
3.45pm: Trampoline (Sam)
Why it suits you: Each of these has a clear next thing to get better at, and the same slot three days running means Saturday's attempt gets to be a correction of Friday's.

7. The adrenaline seeker
10am: Flying trapeze (Hamish and Kirsty)
11.45am: Flying trapeze (Hamish and Kirsty)
2pm: Fly pole (Helen)
3.45pm: Flying trapeze (Gabe and Kirsty)
Why it suits you: Flying trapeze in three of the four slots, and a chance to try Waltzer-dizzying spins on a different fly apparatus. By Sunday you'll know the rig the way other people know their commute. Expect to be exhausted, with shredded hands.

8. The show-off
10am: Tippy hoop (Kerri)
11.45am: Straps and loops (Robyn)
2pm: Silks (Kirsty)
3.45pm: Partner and group acro (Robyn)
Why it suits you: Silks and straps look good from the back of the room, tippy hoop is the most unusual apparatus in the room and group acro brings a cast with its own cheering section.

On Monday, when a colleague asks if you had a good weekend, you may want to suggest they sit down.

BIGGEST OPEN RIG NIGHT YET

38 PERFORMERS + TOP TEAM + HAPPY AUDIENCE = GREAT NIGHT

Open Rig Night began with three-year-old Lydia and her mum Joanne performing a new act together, which Lydia had begun planning during rehearsals for their first one! It's hard to beat the unabashed creativity of a pre-schooler.

The rest of the night kept up – we'll have pro pix to share soon to prove it. For now the flyers can be relied on to capture their own brilliance.

Youth students took a 40-minute segment of their own. The adults included recreational students who had never been on a stage before, and Karen in full Halloween costume and make-up. Then came the pro course students, graduates, and the teachers and artists, with the fly show made up mostly of the Friday-night regulars. That makes 38 performers, and two of them did two acts each.

The audience made the night. They were supportive from the first act to the last, and the feedback so far has been excellent. Not every trick was pulled off to perfection, and the room clapped and cheered all the same.

Putting on a night like that leaves us with a question, and we have not decided anything yet. We want to give everyone the chance to perform. It asks for courage, it is a way to express yourself creatively, it does something for you personally, and it feels so good once you've done it.

Even so, a night like that runs to just short of three hours.

There are a few ways to go. We could put it on two nights in a row or more times a year. We could run specific groups, such as a youth or graduates or fly show. Or there may be a solution none of us has thought of yet. If you have any ideas or opinions, shoot us a DM or an email: info@contact.aerialedge.co.uk
