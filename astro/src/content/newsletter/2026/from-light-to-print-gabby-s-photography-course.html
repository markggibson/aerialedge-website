YOUR SECRET AERIAL PARTNER

LOOKING AT THINGS DIFFERENTLY

When people ask about the foundations of circus performer, they usually expect me to talk about conditioning and flexibility. I’m a big advocate of both. But the real foundation of everything we do in the air is the rigging – the invisible partner has to get it right every single time.

For me, rigging has never been just about steel and cables, mechanics and load calculations. It is about the peace of mind that comes from total technical certainty.

My work in the upcoming rigging workshop and courses – and on our sister website circusrigging.info is to take years of experience on international set-ups and boil it down so guesswork is eliminated and everyone stays safe.

Another sideways step from the obvious in circus is Gabby sharing his expertise in his photography course. Gabby is a great example of the Aerial Edge spirit – he is a cinematographer and flying trapeze teacher who understands that whether you are framing a shot or timing a catch, the principles of focus and intention matter.

Remember that the work you do on the ground is what gives you the freedom to fly.

Mark Gibson

Aerial Edge Founder

FROM LIGHT TO PRINT

A FULL PHOTOGRAPHY JOURNEY

Analogue Photography: From Light to Print is an immersive, hands-on photography course that uses film as a tool to slow down and understand how photographs are made.

Designed and taught by Gabriel Stella, Aerial Edge’s photographer, cinematographer and flying trapeze teacher to name a few of his talents, this course focuses on the core principles of photography – light, exposure, composition, and intention.

Open to complete beginners and experienced photographers alike, the course emphasises practice, experimentation, and discussion, guiding you from first exposure to final print while building skills that apply to all forms of photography, analogue or digital.

You will explore core photography concepts, learn how cameras work, shoot on film, and work in the lab to develop negatives and produce your own darkroom prints.

Over the two four-hour sessions you’ll cover theory, camera basics, film shooting, development, and darkroom printing. You’ll learn photography fundamentals by completing the entire process – from light to print.

THE 4-DAY SHIFT: WHAT TO EXPECT

NAVIGATING THE ARC OF A CIRCUS INTENSIVE

Committing to the four-day Easter Edge festival is a different psychological and physical game than showing up for your regular classes. The intensive is designed to be a challenge that gives you progress – you can come out the other side with a permanent upgrade of your circus skills.

Understanding the rhythm of the four days and how your body processes this kind of immersion can help you get the most out of every session.

You’ll choose four arts that you want to train in 90-minute slots, in the same sequence every day, and each will have different demands on your body and brain (see arts choices below).

Here’s the typical arc of the intensive experience:

  • Day 1 – The information surge: Your first day is about data. You will be introduced to new technical logic and maybe you’ll have chosen specialised disciplines like tippy hoop or doubles trapeze. Today is definitively not about perfection – it is to map out the new movements in your mind.

  • Day 2 – The physical peak: This is often the most demanding day because your muscles are feeling the previous day's effort and your body and mind are also tired from the mental exertion of processing everything. However, your nervous system is starting to recognise the patterns. This is where the compounding effect begins, as you start to build on the foundations you laid 24 hours earlier.

  • Day 3 – The mental breakthrough: Your body is in the zone, and the initial information overload settles. Moves that felt like a puzzle on day one start to feel more like physical paths, your muscles remember so it feels more intuitive. You will find you have more mental space to focus on the fluidity of sequences rather than just the next grip.

  • Day 4 – The integration: The final day is about ownership. You have moved past the learning phase and into a state of fluency in a number of areas. This is the point where you often experience their biggest breakthroughs, successfully executing drops or transitions that have eluded you for months in weekly classes.

To stay resilient throughout the festival, we recommend focusing on high-quality recovery – think hydration, protein, and plenty of sleep.

Arts you could choose from: flying trapeze, static trapeze, doubles trapeze, tippy hoop, hammock, rope, silks, acrobatics including handstands, trampoline, Chinese pole, partner acrobatics, straps.

FIRST, DO NO HARM

THE RIGGER'S RESPONSIBILITY

In medicine, the guiding principle is "first, do no harm." In the circus world, that same weight of responsibility sits on the shoulders of the rigger.

When a circus artist leaves the ground, they are handing over their safety to the equipment and the person who installed it. There is no middle ground – you either get it right, or the consequences can be serious.

While our three-hour workshop is for the bare necessities of rigging, this course is if you want to carry that responsibility with professional-grade confidence. It’s if you need to move beyond "it looks okay" to "I know exactly why this works".

We focus on the specific demands of circus – where a moving body can triple the force on a point in a split second – while covering the theory, practice, laws and regulations of all kinds of rigging.

Moving beyond intuition

We don't rig by guesswork. We rig by known quantities. This course takes the mechanics of circus safety and turns them into a practical, repeatable system.

  • Part 1: Essential Knowledge – This weekend is a standalone deep dive about the science. You’ll learn to figure out dynamic loads (the weight of a body in motion), understand the structural limits of hardware, and master the paperwork that ensures every point is legally and physically sound.

  • Part 2: Advanced Principles – This part builds on the first weekend. It gives you necessary skills for managing complex systems, executing rescues, and understanding how to rig in challenging architectural environments. We cover pulley systems, advanced configurations, and the most critical part of the job: what to do if something goes wrong. Understanding rescue protocols is a fundamental part of the "do no harm" ethos.

Mark Gibson leads these weekends with a focus on clarity and real-world application. He brings world-class industry standards into a practical, doable format. Mark is not only in charge of the rigging at Aerial Edge, he works a consultant to national arts organisations.

These weekends are great for anyone who wants to be the person who can say "yes, this is safe" with confidence in your knowledge to back it up.

You can join us for the first weekend to get solid foundations, or commit to both to gain a comprehensive professional edge. Either way, you will leave with a transformed perspective on what it means to keep a performer safe. And that performer might be you.

THE HALF-TERM RESET

THREE DAYS FOR THE MAGIC TO HAPPEN

There is much more to a young person’s life than school – or at least, there should be. This February half-term, we are offering an antidote to the winter slump.

From Monday 16th February, our doors open for a three-day circus intensive designed to get kids out of their heads and into the air.

Whether they are total beginners or have some gymnastics, dance, cheerleading or parkour experience, this camp is about more than just keeping busy. It’s a fun way to upgrade confidence, coordination, concentration, creativity, collaboration and social connection.

Why it’s so worth it

While school keeps you in a chair, circus keeps you in motion. It is a unique blend of physical challenge and creative play where there are no winners or losers – only personal breakthroughs.

  • The famous flying trapeze – This is the highlight of our camp. There is nothing quite like the rush of a trick on this bar.

  • A world of arts – Students don’t just do one thing. They explore silks, static trapeze, hoop, trampoline, acrobatics, and object manipulation like juggling and hula hooping.

  • The un-school ethos – Our coaches focus on collaboration and support. It’s a space where trying and failing is just part of the fun, and where every child finds an art that makes them feel capable.

The details

This can be a three-day immersive experience, or you can book for a single day to test the waters. Magic happens over the full 72 hours as new skills start to click, friendships form and the reset begins for brains and bodies that are meant to be in motion.
