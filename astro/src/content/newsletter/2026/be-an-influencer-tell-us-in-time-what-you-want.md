## SURPRISE YOURSELF

## ANKIE'S EXPERIENCE ON 4-WEEK INTENSIVE

Ankie signed up for the summer 4-Week Intensive without a fixed plan, but she knew that trampoline she could take or leave, and if she was honest it looked a little dangerous.

Four weeks later it was one of her favourite things in the building. "It makes me feel like a kid again," she said. "I surprised myself, just doing it every time, and seeing progress kind of fast."

That can happen in any of our courses, even in the short Intensive. And that's why we start all three of them with a broad range of arts on the schedule.

You know what your mum said when trying to talk you out of many mini-rebellions that had your arms crossed and your eyebrows heavy: you don't know if you'll like it til you try it.

Ankie says: "I didn't know what to expect, and it was really nice to do something different every day. For example, back-somersaulting in different kinds of apparatus.

"I didn't expect to like acrobatics and trampolining as much as I did. Actually I really enjoyed everything. It's hard to choose one thing that I didn't like!"

You may arrive thinking you know which discipline is yours. But you leave having tested it against the others.

Ankie also confirmed that even on the shortest course, you get a lot out of the group experience.

She says: "I really liked the atmosphere. I felt the group was really close. You get to know people in a very different way, without even talking that much, it's really nice to get to know each other physically.

"All the teachers were very nice, and it felt more like peer-to-peer learning – I didn't feel intimidated by the teachers, which is good.

"I felt very respected and they knew how to change the class according to my level and things I wanted to do. I also liked that they asked to give suggestions on what we wanted to learn and then we could do that in the next class."

And before you know it, you've learned enough to present a short act to friends, family and fellow students on the last afternoon of your course.

Ankie's showing had her on doubles trapeze with fellow student Esther, proving her point that closeness and trust can be built in a short time.

## THIS ISN'T A CLASS ANY MORE

## OPEN RIG NIGHT IS WHEN TRAINING GETS AN AUDIENCE

You may have watched them do this before, or done it yourself. The drill, three failed attempts before the fourth one lands, the look of disbelief when a trick that wasn't there in August is suddenly there in September.

Open Rig Night is where all of that gets an audience. The people you train alongside, or queue behind for the kettle, or nod to on the rig on a Tuesday, get up on the school's stage and turn training into something you can actually sit down and watch.

Expect aerial. Expect acro. Expect at least one thing you've never seen before, and at least one person you know doing something you didn't know they could do.

The last two Open Rig Nights sold out. If you want to be in the room for this one, it's worth booking rather than counting on a spare seat turning up.

## STOP STARTING FROM SCRATCH

## OCTOBER EDGE GIVES YOU THREE DAYS ON THE SAME THING, NOT ONE

Sign up for October Edge curious about something you've never tried, and three days is oddly long enough to go from can't to can, in a discipline you'd never have called yours on the Friday morning.

This year, one of your disciplines could be straps or loops, taught by Robyn.

Robyn is a graduate of our one-year course, and has been a devotee of both of the related arts for a long time. The pic above is from one of her performances of loops.

She's also teaching partner acro at the Edge, as she does in the weekly class.

There's a specific frustration that belongs to one-off workshops or even weekly classes, which you can overcome at the Edge.

You spend an hour getting somewhere with a new grip, a new shape, a new nerve you didn't know you had, and then the session ends. You go home. Life happens. By the time you're back in the building, it could feel like you're starting from the same place you started before.

October Edge doesn't do that to you. Pick a track and you're on it for all three days, same group, same coach, which means you're picking up exactly where you left off. Nothing you learn evaporates overnight, and when you build on that next day, the learning sticks.

And you do this in four time slots every day.  They could be for the first time, or you might have tried them all before at some point. But one thing is for sure, that's four arts you'll make progress in.
